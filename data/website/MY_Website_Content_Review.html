🔬 Research: Architecture

Was Lorem Ipsum NEW content ready

Card Preview (short abstract)

How do buildings shape cognition? My work explores the cognitive and neural mechanisms through which architectural features—ceiling height, natural light, spatial layout, material texture—affect how people think, feel, and behave. This research bridges cognitive science, neuroscience, and architectural design, asking how we can design buildings that actively support human wellbeing, creativity, and social interaction.

Key Findings

Architectural space is 'lightweight reconfigurable'—people reshape spatial ensembles dozens of times daily, unlike fixed digital interfaces
Buildings create 'intimacy gradients' through ceiling height, room size, and natural light that shape our sense of privacy and social openness
The Web of Belief framework maps 180+ design templates from neuroscience findings to specific architectural features with calibrated confidence
Architects think about interactivity as a 3D spatial ensemble rather than a state-space of forced choices
Environmental features affect perception through embodied mechanisms—tools, spaces, and bodily states change what we see

💃 Research: Choreography

Was Lorem Ipsum NEW content ready

Card Preview

Dance is thinking with the body. My research with world-class choreographers—particularly a decade-long collaboration with Wayne McGregor and Random Dance—reveals how expert movers use their bodies as cognitive instruments: marking movements to reduce complexity, distributing memory across dancers, and using physical exploration to generate ideas that could never arise from purely mental deliberation.

Key Findings

Marking in dance is an epistemic action—a way of thinking with the body, not just conserving energy
Choreographic creativity is distributed across multiple dancers' bodies, spatial layout, and sonic environment
Memory for dance is socially distributed—no single dancer holds the entire piece
Physical exploration generates movement ideas that purely mental deliberation cannot
Spatial landmarks in the studio serve as external memory anchors for movement sequences

💡 Research: Creativity

Was Lorem Ipsum NEW content ready

Card Preview

Creativity is not a purely internal flash of insight—it is deeply interactive. My research shows that creative cognition depends on working with external things: sketches, prototypes, instruments, other people. By interacting with materials and ideas in their environment, creative thinkers recruit resources that broaden and deepen their thinking in ways that purely mental deliberation cannot match.

Key Findings

Creative cognition depends on interaction with external materials—sketches, prototypes, instruments, other people
Deep processing is better understood as interactive coupling with external resources than purely internal activation
Chance encounters with environmental side-effects significantly increase creative insight (Duncker's candle problem)
Architects, mathematicians, dancers, and musicians all create through cyclical externalization-reflection loops
The longer and more varied the interaction with materials, the broader the set of resources recruited

🧠 Research: Embodied, Extended, Situated

Was Lorem Ipsum NEW content ready

Card Preview

What does it mean to think with your body—and with the things around you? My work on the 4E's (Embodied, Embedded, Extended, Enactive cognition) explores how tools, environments, and bodily states reshape perception, expand our cognitive capacities, and blur the boundary between mind and world. When we wield a tool, our brain literally incorporates it into our body schema, changing what we perceive and how we act.

Key Findings

Tools change perception—a chisel in hand lets you 'see' where cement should go; a heavy backpack makes hills look steeper
Our body schema can incorporate tools, vehicles, and even virtual body parts, changing perceived reachable space
Genuine cognitive extension requires tight coupling: reliable availability, easy access, and functional integration
Skill with a tool partly determines which affordances are visible—experts detect more tool-relevant features
Goals and attention hyperbolically warp perceived space—we exaggerate what we care about and compress what we don't

🤝 Research: Interactivity

Was Lorem Ipsum NEW content ready

Card Preview

Interactivity is the engine of thought. My theory of interactivity explains how people amplify their cognitive powers by physically manipulating their environment—rearranging Scrabble tiles, gesturing while speaking, sketching while designing. These are not idle fidgets; they are epistemic actions that make thinking faster, more reliable, and more creative than pure mental deliberation alone.

Key Findings

Epistemic actions—actions that change the world to simplify thought—are as fundamental as pragmatic actions
Rearranging Scrabble tiles, gesturing while talking, and mise en place are all interactive cognition
Interactive cognition is more powerful than detached deliberation: offloads memory, reduces complexity, creates new representations
ACM Notable Publication 2013 for 'Embodied Cognition and the Magical Future of Interaction Design'
Practical implications for HCI, architecture, dance, and educational design

🗺️ Research: Planning

Was Lorem Ipsum NEW content ready

Card Preview

How do people plan in complex, dynamic environments? My early work at MIT's AI Lab investigated how intelligent agents plan actions in real-time when the world changes unpredictably. This research bridged artificial intelligence and cognitive science, showing that effective planning requires continuous interaction with the environment rather than complete mental simulation before acting.

Key Findings

Effective planning requires interleaving planning and execution rather than complete mental simulation
Exploratory actions that reveal environmental information are as important as goal-directed actions
Environmental structure simplifies planning—people exploit spatial layout to reduce search
Anticipates the theory of epistemic actions: intelligence emerges from agent-environment coupling
Grounds later work on how architects use sketches and models as externalized planning tools

📊 Research: Representation

Was Lorem Ipsum NEW content ready

Card Preview

How do external representations—diagrams, notations, maps, models—shape thought? My research investigates why some representations make problems easy and others make them hard, and how the choice of representational format affects what people can see, infer, and discover. This work connects cognitive science to practical questions about information design, data visualization, and architectural drawing.

Key Findings

Representational format profoundly affects problem-solving speed, accuracy, and the insights people can reach
Diagrams support different inferences than sentential representations—making spatial relationships visible
People actively modify external representations as a problem-solving strategy (representational tinkering)
Representational choice is an epistemic action—changing the display changes what you can think
Practical implications for information architecture, data visualization, and notation design

👁️ Research: Perceptual Thinking

Was Lorem Ipsum NEW content ready

Card Preview

Can you think with your eyes? My research on perceptual thinking investigates how visual and spatial reasoning go beyond passive seeing. When experts look at a chess board, an architectural plan, or a mathematical diagram, they are not just registering what's there—they are actively projecting, imagining, and reasoning through what they see. Perception itself is a form of cognition.

Key Findings

Perception is an active cognitive process—experts see possibilities, threats, and affordances, not just objects
Projection allows people to mentally overlay imagined alternatives onto perceived scenes
Physical thinking (forces, balance) and visual thinking (spatial transformation) engage different neural resources
Design cognition fundamentally depends on projecting alternatives onto current perceptions
Implications for visualization tools, architectural drawing, and educational design

🔒 Research: Implicit/Explicit

Was Lorem Ipsum NEW content ready

Card Preview

Much of what we know, we know without knowing that we know it. My research on implicit and explicit cognition explores the boundary between conscious, deliberate thought and the vast unconscious processes that shape our behavior. How much of skilled performance is explicitly planned versus implicitly guided? When can implicit knowledge be made explicit—and does doing so help or hinder performance?

Key Findings

Skilled performance depends on implicit motor and perceptual knowledge that cannot be fully verbalized
Making implicit knowledge explicit sometimes helps (teaching) and sometimes hurts (overthinking)
Buildings affect cognition through implicit channels—ceiling height, light, materials operate below awareness
Creative breakthroughs often involve sudden explicit recognition of implicitly known patterns
Epistemic actions work partly by making implicit possibilities explicit—rearranging reveals hidden structure
