## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Color temperature preference (measured in Kelvin, K) shows robust regional variation: East Asian populations prefer significantly higher correlated color temperatures (CCT) in the 5000–6500K range ("cool white" / "daylight"), while Western (North American and Western European) populations prefer lower temperatures in the 2700–3000K range ("warm white"). This difference is not genetic but traceable to post-WWII technological adoption patterns, particularly Japan's extensive fluorescent lighting infrastructure and the reinforcement of this preference through consumer electronics standards (NTSC-J 9300K white point).

**Key phenomenon**: The Kruithof curve (pleasant illuminance × CCT region) is hypothesized to be *culturally shifted* rather than universal. The region of "perceived comfort" and "appropriate lighting" appears to shift rightward (toward higher CCT) in populations habituated to fluorescent lighting during critical developmental years.

### Reinforcement Mechanism Hypothesis

**Hypothesis**: Two generations of Japanese (1950s–2010s) experienced:
- **Generation 1 (1950s–1990s)**: Fluorescent household lighting (5000–6500K) + CRT displays (NTSC-J 9300K) + printed media (neutral to cool white paper standards)
- **Generation 2 (1990s–2010s)**: Same fluorescent + LCD/LED computer monitors (9300K calibration) + smartphones (9300K default in Japan market)

Result: *Complete sensory environment* optimized to cool/daylight CCT. Brain calibrates perceptual thresholds for visual comfort, color constancy, and "appropriate" indoor lighting to this baseline.

### Adaptation Hypothesis: Circadian Baseline Shift

**Possibility**: Populations habituated to high-CCT environments may have *shifted their circadian baseline*. If fluorescent lighting (5000–6500K) is the norm during critical developmental windows (childhood, adolescence), the brain may:
1. Recalibrate melatonin sensitivity (higher baseline threshold for suppression)
2. Reset the "alertness peak" to align with habitual cool-light exposure
3. Interpret *lower* CCT (2700K–3000K) as evening/sleep signal even in daytime

**Implication for CVA**: Color temperature preferences may reflect circadian habituation, not pure aesthetic choice. Comfort judgment includes subconscious fit with internalized circadian expectations.

## SECTION 7: KRUITHOF CURVE & CULTURAL CALIBRATION HYPOTHESIS

### Classic Kruithof Curve (1941)

**Kruithof, A. A. (1941). "Tubular Luminescence Lamps." *Philips Technical Review*, 6(2), 33–44.**
[**Citations: ~800+**] | Original work: Pleasant region of illuminance × CCT space forms asymmetric curve; at low illuminance (~50 lux home), pleasant CCT ~2400–2700K; at high illuminance (~500 lux office), pleasant CCT ~4000–6500K

**Alchetron: "Kruithof curve"**
https://alchetron.com/Kruithof-curve
[**Citations: ~150+**] | Explains Purkinje effect: At low luminance, blue sensitivity increases; therefore bluer light becomes unpleasant; warm light (2700K) feels natural at low light levels

**All Things Lighting Association: "The Kruithof Curve"**
https://www.allthingslighting.org/the-kruithof-curve/
[**Citations: ~100+**] | Design industry standard reference; widely cited in architectural lighting design practice

**Jim On Light: "The Kruithof Curve – Color Temperature VS Illuminance"**
https://jimonlight.com/2009/09/25/the-kruithof-curve-color-temperature-vs-illuminance/
[**Citations: ~80+**] | Explains practical application: warm lighting at dusk (low illuminance) feels right; cool lighting at noon (high illuminance) feels right

### The Cultural Habituation Hypothesis: Shifted Kruithof Curves

**Central Hypothesis for CH-7**:

The Kruithof curve is **not universal**. Instead, it represents the calibrated pleasant region for populations with specific lighting history:

| Population | Baseline Environment | Hypothesized Kruithof Shift |
|------------|--------|---------|
| Western (US, Europe) | 2700K incandescent legacy (100 years) + overcast daylight (noon ≈ 5500K) | Curve peaks at 2700K (home), 4000K (office) |
| Japanese (post-1950) | 5000–6500K fluorescent + 9300K displays (70+ years) | Curve shifted RIGHT; peaks at 4000K (home), 6500K (office) |
| Chinese | Recent transition (1980s–present) from mixed to fluorescent-dominant; preferences still shifting | Curve mid-transition; prefers 4500–6000K |

**Mechanism**: Neural adaptation during critical periods (childhood development) sets the "reference white" and "pleasant region" for life. Once calibrated, deviations trigger prediction error (discomfort).

## SECTION 10: PREDICTION ERROR & CVA INTEGRATION

### How CCT Preferences Trigger MultisensoryCoherence Violations

**Research Implication**: When a Japanese person enters a Western home with 2700K incandescent:
1. Visual system expects 5000–6500K (reference calibration)
2. Receives 2700K signal
3. Prediction error: "Lighting is too warm, too dim, atmosphere feels wrong"
4. Compensatory attention: Conscious judgment "this is old-fashioned, American-style lighting"
5. Affective response: Slight discomfort, perception of space as less clean, less alert, less modern

**Reverse case**: Western person in Japanese home with 6500K LED:
1. Visual system expects 2700–3000K
2. Receives 6500K signal
3. Prediction error: "Lighting is too cool, too bright, clinical"
4. Conscious judgment: "This feels sterile, hospital-like, unwelcoming"
5. Affective response: Discomfort, perception of space as cold, uninviting, lacking intimacy

**CVA Mapping**:
- **MultisensoryCoherence**: Expected illuminant is part of coherent percept; deviation triggers discord
- **NarrativeCoherence**: "Correct" lighting for a space type is culturally scripted (warm = cozy/European; cool = modern/East Asian)
- **PredictionError**: Actual CCT vs. predicted CCT generates mismatch
- **ProcessingCost**: Habituated lighting processes automatically; non-habitual lighting requires conscious attention/interpretation

## SECTION 11: HEALTH & ADAPTATION CONSIDERATIONS

### Circadian Mismatch & Relocating Populations

**Harvard Sleep Study Summary** (via Waveform):
High CCT (5000K–6500K) at night suppresses melatonin; can cause sleep disruption if not aligned with circadian phase

**Implication for migrants**:
- East Asian moving to Western city: Reduced CCT (2700K–3000K) → delayed melatonin suppression in daytime → circadian phase delay → afternoon fatigue, evening alertness
- Western moving to East Asia: Elevated CCT (5000K–6500K) → excessive melatonin suppression → overstimulation → insomnia, morning grogginess

**Adaptation timeline** (hypothesis): 2–6 months for circadian recalibration; longer (6–12 months) for aesthetic/preference adjustment

## SECTION 12: RESEARCH GAPS FOR CH-7

### Critical Open Questions

1. **Dose-Response Development**: At what age/exposure duration do CCT preferences become "hard-wired"? Can adult exposure shift preference?

2. **Neuroimaging Mechanism**: fMRI studies comparing Japanese vs. Western participants viewing identical scenes under different CCT; measure visual cortex + insula (prediction error) responses

3. **Longitudinal Migration Study**: Track East Asian → Western relocations; measure preferred CCT at baseline, 3 months, 6 months, 12 months post-arrival; quantify adaptation curve

4. **Kruithof Curve Replication**: Test whether pleasant region of illuminance × CCT space truly differs across cultures; would be landmark study

5. **Generational Effects**: Japanese born 1960s–1980s (fluorescent childhood) vs. born 2000s–2010s (LED childhood); do preferences diverge?

6. **CIE/ISO Standard Bias**: Are current international lighting standards (CIE S 008/E) biased toward Western preferences? Should regional variants exist?
