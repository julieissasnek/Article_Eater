## 1. PROJECT IDENTITY

**Article Eater** — A system for expert evaluation of scientific papers about architecture and human wellbeing. Core method: expert panels produce mechanistic templates that explain how built environments affect human psychology and behavior through a Predictive Processing (PE) framework. Each template formalizes a specific mechanism (e.g., how fractal patterns reduce visual processing load, how spatial integration predicts social encounter frequency) with causal links, scope conditions, moderators, and key references.

**Principal**: David — professor of cognitive science at UCSD (35 years), former MIT AI Lab research faculty. Prefers clean academic writing (Bertrand Russell style without exaggerated Britishisms), APA references with Google Scholar citation counts, longer detailed responses, and clear marking of scientific support strength.

## 7. KEY CROSS-TEMPLATE PATTERNS DISCOVERED THIS SESSION

### Multi-Channel Convergence as General Principle

Three templates now share the same structural pattern: a natural stimulus engages 5+ PE channels simultaneously, producing super-additive convergent benefits.

- **L3** (Daylight): visual, circadian, temporal, spectral, spatial channels
- **MAT4** (Wood): visual, haptic, olfactory, acoustic, biophilic channels
- **VIEW1** (Nature view): fractal, prospect, attention-restoration, temporal, ecological-safety channels

This convergence principle may be the deepest insight of the template system: natural stimuli are consistently more beneficial than synthetic equivalents because they confirm predictions across MORE channels simultaneously.

### Olfaction as Transition Modality

OLF1 establishes that smell encodes arrivals and changes (not steady states) due to rapid adaptation. This makes olfaction a natural partner to TP2 (episodic boundaries) and SC3 (promenade thresholds) — it adds a uniquely memorial modality at exactly the spatial transitions that matter most for architectural experience.

### The Open-Plan Office as Case Study

SOC-I produced the most complete mechanistic account of why open-plan offices produce dissatisfaction: they maximize SC4 encounter benefits while simultaneously eliminating ALL FIVE privacy mechanisms (SOC2) and collapsing the territorial gradient (SOC3). The design solution is not all-private or all-open but a GRADIENT of social exposure settings matching Dunbar's social network layers.

### Color as Modest Contributor

COL-I's most important finding may be corrective: color effects are real but smaller, more context-dependent, and more subject to habituation than popular claims suggest. The most robust effect is arousal modulation through saturation/brightness (partially subcortical, resistant to habituation). Meaning-based color effects (ecological valence, cultural associations) are genuine but context-dependent and they habituate in sustained-occupancy settings.
