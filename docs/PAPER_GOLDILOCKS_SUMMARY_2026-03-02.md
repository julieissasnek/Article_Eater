The paper explicitly distinguishes evidence levels:

### Very Strong Evidence (d = 0.50–0.60)
- Thermal comfort inverted-U (de Dear & Brager 1998; Cabanac 1971)

### Strong Evidence (d = 0.35–0.40)
- Visual complexity optimization at fractal D ≈ 1.3–1.5
- Cultural variation in C* (Japanese vs. German; Redies et al. 2020)

### Moderate Evidence (d = 0.20–0.35)
- Acoustic preference peaks at 50–60 dB LAeq (Axelsson et al. 2010)
- Social density optimum at 3–5 groups (Dunbar 1992; limited replication)
- Temporal variation effects

### Gaps (Requiring Future Research)
- Olfactory Goldilocks zone (no controlled studies)
- Cross-modal interactions (thermal modulating visual complexity tolerance)
- Developmental trajectories (children vs. adults)
- Cross-cultural generalization beyond WEIRD samples
- Functional form validation (Gaussian vs. alternatives)
- Cross-modal universality mechanism

## MATHEMATICAL FORMALIZATION

The paper introduces the core equation:

**P(x) = exp(−(C(x) − C*)²/(2σ(ψ)²))**

Where:
- **P(x)** = Preference for stimulus x (0–1 scale)
- **C(x)** = Objective complexity measure (fractal dimension, entropy, decibels, etc.)
- **C*** = Optimal complexity (domain- and individual-specific)
- **σ(ψ)** = Bandwidth of tolerance (reflects expertise, uncertainty, personality)

**Scope Conditions**:
1. Neurotypical adult populations (optima differ in neurodivergent individuals, children)
2. Steady-state exposure (transient stimuli may not reach equilibrium preference)
3. Bounded attention (visual field contains ≤6 visual objects/groups)
4. Single-modality measurement (cross-modal interactions partially unmapped)

**Justification for Gaussian Form**:
- Parsimonious (two free parameters)
- Empirically fits published data across domains
- Theoretically justified by symmetric prediction error minimization
- Alternative forms (skewed Gaussian, Weibull) may fit better; requires empirical validation

## THEORETICAL CONTRIBUTIONS

### Primary Contribution: Cross-Modal Universality
The principle that all sensory modalities (visual, thermal, acoustic, temporal, social) optimize under the same prediction-error efficiency framework. This is not derivable from component T1 frameworks but is empirically observable across domains.

### Secondary Contributions:
1. **Formal Specification**: First mathematical model of inverted-U preferences with two interpretable parameters
2. **Neural Implementation**: Maps principle onto brain systems (V1/V2 for visual, insula for thermal, STS for social, etc.)
3. **Mechanistic Reduction**: Shows how four T1 frameworks (PP, IC, NM, IE-DPT) contribute complementary mechanistic understanding
4. **Architectural Operationalization**: Provides actionable design targets (D = 1.2–1.5 visual, 50–60 dB acoustic, etc.)
5. **Cultural Framework**: Explains C* variation through visual diet hypothesis and Bayesian prior updating
