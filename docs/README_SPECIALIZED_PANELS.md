## Panel Structure

### PANEL A: Cross-Cultural Valuation Structure (Barrett's Objection)

**Question**: Are the nine CVA valuation axes culturally universal or Western theoretical impositions?

**Three Panelists × Three Panels = 9 Cultural Experts**

#### A1: Japanese Aesthetics Panel
- **Members**: Akira Hasegawa (environmental psychology, ma), Yuki Nakamura (social psychology, amae), Hiroshi Yamamoto (architecture, wabi-sabi)
- **Key Finding**: Axes are context-dependent; AutonomySupport may be negative value; missing SeasonalAlignment and EthicalPresence axes
- **Verdict**: Structure varies; not culturally universal

#### A2: West African Aesthetics Panel
- **Members**: Adeyemi Adeniyil (architecture, Yoruba), Oluwatoyin Adebayo (aesthetics, àṣà), Chiké Okonkwo (social psychology, communal self-construal)
- **Key Finding**: Status + Belonging + Identity + Relatedness form **inseparable cluster**; cannot be independently weighted
- **Verdict**: Four axes are structurally coupled

#### A3: Indian Classical Aesthetics Panel
- **Members**: Ravi Kshirsagar (classical aesthetics, rasa theory), Priya Sharma (architecture, vastu shastra), Ashok Desai (psychology, non-Western aesthetics)
- **Key Finding**: Aesthetic experience is **irreducibly holistic**; rasa theory provides alternative decomposition (9 rasas, not 9 valuations)
- **Verdict**: CVA is one instantiation; rasa is another; incommensurable

**Section Length**: ~400 lines
**Key Tables**: Cross-cultural comparison (3×5 scenarios × 3 panels = 45 detailed scenario ratings)

### PANEL B: Architectural Utility (Zumthor's Objection)

**Question**: Can CVA help architects CREATE novel designs, or does formalization constrain creativity?

**Four Architect-Thinkers**:
1. Juhani Pallasmaa (phenomenological architecture)
2. Kengo Kuma (material dissolution, anti-object)
3. Diébédo Francis Kéré (participatory design)
4. Elizabeth Diller (conceptual architecture)

**Three Design Problems**:
1. **Meditation Center in Downtown Manhattan** — Restore amid chaos
2. **Children's Hospital** — Simultaneous SafetyValue + CompetenceSupportValue + InterestValue
3. **Multicultural Community Center** — Support BelongingValue + IdentityCongruenceValue across 12 cultures

**Method**:
- (a) Architects design intuitively (without CVA)
- (b) CVA decomposition shown
- (c) Architects state whether CVA changes approach

**Findings**:
- CVA useful for **constraint articulation** and trade-off analysis
- CVA constrains **radical creativity** (designers sometimes want to reject optimization)
- CVA missing **social, phenomenological, and temporal dimensions**

**Section Length**: ~300 lines

### PANEL C: Structural Universality (Kitayama's Objection)

**Question**: Does the STRUCTURE of valuation space change across cultures, or just the weights?

**Method**: Sorting Task
- Take the 9 CVA axes
- Each panel reorganizes them to reflect their culture
- Compare structures

**Results**:
- **Japanese**: Hierarchical (foundational > contextual > orthogonal)
- **West African**: Clustered (four axes inseparable)
- **Indian**: Rasa-based (holistic states, not dimensions)

**Cross-Cultural Comparison Table**:
- Hierarchy structure: Japanese (Yes), West African (No), Indian (Yes) → **NO CONSENSUS**
- Axis independence: Japanese (Partial), West African (No), Indian (No) → **MOSTLY NO**
- Individual autonomy: Japanese (Absent), West African (Absent), Indian (Absent) → **CONSENSUS: NOT A VALUE**
- Collective frame: All three → **CONSENSUS: FUNDAMENTAL**

## Key Findings at a Glance

### What the Panels Discovered

**Panel A**: The nine axes are **not culturally universal**. Different cultures structure valuations differently. Some cluster axes (West Africa), some make them hierarchical (Japan), some use incommensurable frameworks (India/rasa).

**Panel B**: CVA is useful as a **constraint-analysis tool** but not as a **design generator**. Architects should use it to identify trade-offs, not to prescribe solutions.

**Panel C**: The **structure** of valuation space, not just weights, is culturally contingent. This is more fundamental than Kitayama initially claimed.

**Panel D**: **No fatal mathematical flaws**. All remaining gaps are solvable. Three require additional work; none require abandoning CVA.
