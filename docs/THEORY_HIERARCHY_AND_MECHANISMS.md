# Article Eater: Theory Hierarchy and Mechanisms

**Version 1.1 — February 20, 2026**

This document maps the complete theoretical structure of Article Eater:
- **Tier 1**: 10 neurally-grounded framework theories
- **Tier 1.5**: Domain theories (ART, SRT, Biophilia) with reduction mappings
- **Tier 2**: ~150 CMR templates encoding specific mechanisms
- **Bridge Warrants**: Explicit transfer assumptions licensing cross-domain inference

---

## TIER 1: CANONICAL FRAMEWORK THEORIES (10)

These are neurally grounded, cross-domain frameworks. Every architectural effect must operate through one or more of these. Each satisfies three criteria:
1. **Mechanistic specificity** — connects to neural implementation
2. **Cross-domain generativity** — predictions across multiple domains
3. **Convergent multi-method support** — fMRI, EEG, lesion, computational, behavioral evidence

---

### PP — PREDICTIVE PROCESSING

**Core Mechanism**: The brain continuously generates predictions about sensory input; mismatches (prediction errors) drive learning and attention allocation.

**Neural Substrates**:
- Hierarchical cortical processing (V1 → IT cortex)
- Anterior cingulate cortex (error monitoring)
- Prefrontal cortex (precision weighting)

**Key Principle**: Efficient coding — familiar/expected stimuli processed more efficiently, freeing cognitive resources.

**Neural Substrates**:

- Anterior cingulate cortex (conflict monitoring)

**Key Principle**: Environmental features can bias which system dominates; high arousal/threat → System 1.

## BRIDGE WARRANTS

Bridge warrants are **explicit assumptions that license the transfer of knowledge from one domain to another**. They are load-bearing epistemic structures that determine whether evidence transfers across domains.

The term originates in Nancy Cartwright's work on nomological machines (1999) and has been developed in the context of evidence-based policy (Cartwright & Hardie, 2012).

---

### Why Bridge Warrants Matter

Without bridge warrants, we cannot know whether:
- A finding about **object perception** applies to **room perception**
- A result from **photographs** transfers to **real buildings**
- An effect in **lab settings** generalizes to **ecological contexts**

Bridge warrants make these transfer assumptions **explicit and evaluable**.

---

### Bridge Types (Cartwright Typology, Extended)

| Type | Definition | Default P(bridge) | Example |
|------|------------|-------------------|---------|
| **CONSTITUTIVE** | Target literally contains source | 0.75 | Room corners ARE composed of angular objects |
| **MECHANISM** | Same causal pathway in both domains | 0.60 | Amygdala activation for angular objects → angular rooms |
| **EMPIRICAL_COVARIANCE** | Co-tested in same study | 0.60 | Study measured both photo and in-situ responses |
| **FUNCTIONAL** | Same outcome, different mechanisms | 0.50 | Objects and rooms both evoke negative affect (different pathways) |
| **CAPACITY** | Entity has stable capacity (not mechanism-based) | 0.45 | "Plants have the capacity to reduce stress" |
| **ANALOGICAL** | Structural similarity suggests similar effects | 0.35 | Fractal patterns in nature → fractal patterns in architecture |

---

### Bridge-Weighted Credence Formula

The probability of a CNFA (Cognitive Neuroscience of Architecture) effect is computed as:

```
P(CNFA effect) = P(parent theory) × P(bridge) × P(CNFA-specific evidence)
```

**Example**:
- Parent theory (PP: angular objects → amygdala): P = 0.85
- Bridge (mechanism transfer to rooms): P = 0.60
- CNFA-specific evidence (angular rooms → stress): P = 0.70

**Result**: P(angular rooms → stress) = 0.85 × 0.60 × 0.70 = **0.36**

The bridge is often the weakest link in the evidential chain.

---

### Bridge Lifecycle

```
    HYPOTHESIZED ─────┬────► SUPPORTED (evidence_for grows)
    (machine-proposed)│
                      ├────► CONTESTED (both evidence_for AND evidence_against)
                      │
                      ├────► FAILED (strong disconfirming evidence)
                      │
                      └────► REVISED (downgraded to different type)
```

### Bridge Warrants in the Web of Belief

Bridge warrants are **first-class nodes** in the web, not mere annotations. They:

1. **Connect** theoretical propositions to empirical findings
2. **Carry** their own confidence scores
3. **Can be challenged** by disconfirming evidence
4. **Propagate** uncertainty through the network

```
┌─────────────────────┐         ┌─────────────────────┐
```

### Bridge Warrant Challenges

The system automatically generates challenges to bridge warrants:

| Challenge Type | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Scale mismatch** | Object-level findings may not scale to room-level |
| **Context dependency** | Lab effects may not transfer to ecological contexts |
| **Mechanism divergence** | Different neural pathways may be engaged |
| **Temporal dynamics** | Brief exposures ≠ prolonged habitation |
| **Individual differences** | Population-level findings may not apply to subgroups |
