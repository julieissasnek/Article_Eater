# PANEL TRANSCRIPT

## Panel Charge

Nine templates spanning multisensory material perception in architectural context require calibration. The domain covers the perceptual integration of architectural materials through multiple sensory channels — vision, touch, audition, olfaction — and the affective, cognitive, and cultural processes that modulate material experience. This panel addresses how the brain combines information from different senses to form a unified percept of architectural materials, how that percept generates affective responses, and how cultural learning modulates innate material preferences.

Four critical dependencies constrain this panel's work:

1. **VISUAL-I T1 (Fractal/Texture Statistics)**: T1 owns the visual channel of material perception — fractal dimension, texture statistics, spectral slope. MULTI-I does NOT re-calibrate the visual channel. MULTI-I owns the multi-channel convergence bonus that accrues when visual fractal/texture statistics are accompanied by congruent haptic and olfactory signals from natural materials. The builder AI computes: visual channel effect (from VISUAL-I T1) + convergence bonus (from MULTI-I NATURAL_MATERIAL_CONVERGENCE_001).

2. **Scope correction for CROSSMODAL_CONGRUENCE_001**: The Sprint Task Brief description ("spatial navigation and social cognitive integration") is erroneous. CROSSMODAL_CONGRUENCE_001 concerns multi-sensory spatial coherence — the degree to which visual, auditory, haptic, and olfactory information from the architectural environment are spatially aligned and temporally congruent, producing a unified percept of place. This corrected interpretation is consistent with MSI and SN T1 frameworks and avoids overlap with SOCIAL-I.

3. **STRESS-I T6/T7**: Natural material stress reduction (NATURAL_MATERIAL_CONVERGENCE_001) interacts with T6 (cortisol) and T7 (allostatic anticipation). The convergence bonus operates via parasympathetic activation, which is antagonistic to the stress-I cortisol pathway.

4. **Cultural specificity**: MATERIAL_CULTURAL_CONDITIONING_001 parameters are inherently population-dependent. No universal cultural conditioning parameters exist.

Five calibration disputes are expected:

1. **CT afferent architectural translation**: Can laboratory forearm-stroking data be translated to architectural surface contact (hand on wood railing, foot on stone floor)?
2. **Inverse effectiveness threshold**: At what level of unisensory degradation does multisensory compensation become architecturally significant?
3. **Convergence bonus magnitude**: Is multi-channel natural material stress reduction super-additive, additive, or sub-additive relative to single-channel effects?
4. **Bayesian material integration**: Does Ernst's maximum-likelihood estimation model apply to complex architectural material perception?
5. **Patina empirical grounding**: Can Pallasmaa's phenomenological claims about temporal depth be empirically calibrated?

## ROUND TABLE PHASE

### Opening Statement: Charles Spence — Crossmodal Correspondences and the Architecture of Congruency

There is a systematic structure to cross-sensory perception that architects can exploit. My laboratory has documented over sixty crossmodal correspondences — non-arbitrary associations between stimulus dimensions across different senses (Spence, 2011). High-pitched sounds are associated with bright, angular, elevated stimuli; low-pitched sounds with dark, rounded, lower stimuli. Smooth textures correspond to sweet tastes and high pitches; rough textures to bitter tastes and low pitches. These correspondences are partly innate (present in infants) and partly learned, but they are remarkably consistent across cultures.

For MSI_CONGRUENCY_PRINCIPLE_001, the architectural implication is that congruent multisensory environments — where visual, auditory, haptic, and olfactory properties align along the expected correspondence dimensions — produce processing fluency and positive affect. A room with warm wood walls (visual warmth, haptic warmth, olfactory wood scent) paired with warm lighting and soft acoustic absorption is crossmodally congruent and is processed fluently. A room with warm wood walls under cold fluorescent lighting and hard reverberant acoustics is crossmodally incongruent and produces disfluency.

For CROSSMODAL_CONGRUENCE_001, I will calibrate the spatial coherence dimension: when visual, auditory, and haptic space are aligned (sounds come from where visual sources are, haptic surface properties match visual expectations), the brain generates a unified spatial percept. Misalignment produces spatial disorientation and negative affect.

### Opening Statement: India Morrison — CT Afferents and the Neural Pathway for Pleasant Touch

The C-tactile afferent system is the body's dedicated channel for encoding the pleasantness of touch. CT afferents are unmyelinated, slowly conducting nerve fibres found in hairy skin that respond optimally to gentle, stroking touch at velocities of ~1–10 cm/s, with a peak at approximately 3 cm/s (Löken et al., 2009; Morrison et al., 2010). Unlike Aβ mechanoreceptors that encode discriminative touch (texture, pressure, vibration), CT afferents encode affective valence — whether a touch FEELS pleasant.

For CT_AFFECTIVE_TOUCH_001, the mechanism chain is: architectural surface contact → CT afferent activation (dependent on surface properties: warmth, softness, texture grain) → posterior insular cortex processing → hedonic valuation. The critical architectural translation challenge is that my data are primarily from controlled forearm stroking. Architectural touch involves different body sites (palms, fingertips, feet, back), different contact modes (gripping, pressing, walking, leaning), and different exploration velocities. CT afferent density varies by body site — hairy skin of the forearm has higher CT density than glabrous skin of the palm, though recent evidence suggests CT-like responses in glabrous skin as well (Watkins et al., 2021).

### Opening Statement: Francis McGlone — Surface Material Properties and Haptic Affect

I extend Morrison's CT pathway to the specific problem of architectural material perception. My laboratory has measured the relationship between surface properties — roughness (Ra), thermal conductivity (λ), compliance (Young's modulus E) — and hedonic ratings during haptic exploration (McGlone et al., 2014). The key findings are: (a) moderate roughness (Ra ≈ 1–10 μm) is preferred over very smooth (<0.5 μm, perceived as artificial) or very rough (>50 μm, perceived as unpleasant); (b) thermal conductivity matters enormously — warm-conducting materials (wood λ ≈ 0.12 W/m·K) produce more pleasant touch than cold-conducting materials (steel λ ≈ 50 W/m·K, stone λ ≈ 2–3 W/m·K); (c) moderate compliance (yielding slightly under finger pressure) is preferred over both rigid and soft extremes.

For HAP_SURFACE_MATERIAL_001, these material properties map directly to architectural surface specifications. Wood is the optimal architectural material for pleasant haptic experience: moderate roughness, warm thermal conductivity, moderate compliance. Stone and metal are haptically aversive at room temperature but can be modulated by thermal management (heated handrails, thermally insulated stone seats).

### Opening Statement: Benjamin Rowland — Inverse Effectiveness and Architectural Multisensory Design

The principle of inverse effectiveness (Stein & Meredith, 1993; Rowland & Stein, 2014) states that multisensory enhancement is greatest when the constituent unisensory signals are weakest. When a visual signal is strong and unambiguous, adding a congruent auditory signal adds little. When the visual signal is degraded (dim lighting, fog, distance), adding a congruent auditory signal produces a LARGE enhancement — the multisensory response exceeds the sum of the individual unisensory responses (super-additivity).

For MSI_INVERSE_EFFECTIVENESS_002, the architectural translation is immediate and practically important: multisensory design matters MOST in degraded environments. In well-lit, visually rich spaces, haptic and auditory material cues add modest enhancement. In dim corridors, at night, in foggy conditions, or for visually impaired occupants, haptic and auditory cues become critical for environmental perception. The builder AI should weight multisensory material specifications most heavily in conditions where any single sensory channel is degraded.

### Opening Statement: Marc Ernst — Bayesian Material Integration

The brain is an optimal cue integrator for material perception. When you see and touch a surface simultaneously, your percept of its roughness is not an average of the visual and haptic estimates — it is a reliability-weighted combination that is MORE precise than either estimate alone (Ernst & Banks, 2002). The visual estimate of roughness is weighted by its reliability (inverse variance), and the haptic estimate is weighted by its reliability. The combined estimate has lower variance than either component, following the maximum-likelihood estimation (MLE) principle.

For MATERIAL_IDENTITY_INTEGRATION_001, the architectural implication is that multi-modal material experience (seeing AND touching a wood surface) produces a more confident, more precise material percept than visual-only experience (seeing wood behind glass) or haptic-only experience (touching wood in darkness). The precision gain is predicted by the MLE model: σ_combined² = 1/(1/σ_visual² + 1/σ_haptic²). For typical reliability ratios, visual-haptic integration improves material discrimination precision by ~30–40%.

I must note a constraint from the pre-panel review: my MLE data are from simple laboratory stimuli (gratings, ridged surfaces). The extension to complex architectural materials embedded in spatial context is untested. I will honour the FUNCTIONAL (0.50) maximum for architectural parameters.

### Opening Statement: Qing Li — Phytoncides and the Olfactory Pathway of Natural Material Stress Reduction

My forest bathing research has identified a specific olfactory mechanism by which natural materials reduce stress. Wood-derived volatile organic compounds — phytoncides, principally α-pinene, d-limonene, and β-pinene — are released by wood surfaces, especially fresh or unfinished wood. Exposure to these compounds at concentrations found in forested environments (50–200 μg/m³) produces measurable physiological changes: NK cell activity increases by approximately 52%, urinary adrenaline decreases by 30%, and salivary cortisol decreases by approximately 15% (Li et al., 2009; Li, 2010).

For NATURAL_MATERIAL_CONVERGENCE_001, the olfactory channel provides one of the three convergent pathways (visual + haptic + olfactory) through which natural materials reduce stress. The critical finding is that the olfactory channel operates BELOW conscious awareness — occupants in rooms with natural wood do not typically report smelling wood, yet their physiological stress markers are lower. This is an unconscious parasympathetic pathway, not a conscious preference.

### Opening Statement: Byron Mikellides — Cultural Conditioning of Material Evaluations

I must introduce an uncomfortable complication for the builder AI: material preferences are not universal. My research on evaluative associations with architectural materials demonstrates that the same material evokes systematically different affective responses across cultural contexts (Mikellides, 1990). Marble connotes luxury, permanence, and authority in Mediterranean and Middle Eastern contexts; in Nordic contexts, it connotes coldness, sterility, and institutional power. Concrete connotes brutalism and oppression in post-Soviet contexts; in Japanese modernist tradition, it connotes refined simplicity (Andō's "spiritual concrete").

For MATERIAL_CULTURAL_CONDITIONING_001, ALL parameters must carry a population specifier. There are no universal cultural conditioning coefficients. The template must specify the cultural context for which the parameters are calibrated and provide explicit machinery for the builder AI to select the appropriate cultural parameter set based on the building's location and intended user population.

### Opening Statement: Juhani Pallasmaa — The Haptic Imagination and the Architecture of Aging Materials

Architecture is an art of the body, not the eye. My lifetime argument has been that the reduction of architecture to visual experience — the hegemony of the ocular — impoverishes both the architect's design vocabulary and the occupant's spatial experience. Materials that can be touched, smelled, heard underfoot, felt thermally against the skin, engage the full sensorium and produce what I call the "haptic imagination" — a bodily identification with the built environment.

For MATERIAL_AGING_TEMPORAL_DEPTH_001, I advance a specific claim: materials that age visibly — that accumulate patina, wear marks, weathering patterns — communicate temporal depth and human use-history. A worn stone threshold communicates generations of passage. A patinated bronze handrail communicates thousands of hands. This temporal information is perceptually available and affectively meaningful: it connects the present occupant to a continuum of human experience, producing what I have called "the architecture of time."

I acknowledge the constraint from the pre-panel review: my claims are phenomenological and philosophical. I cannot provide confidence scores or bridge warrants from my own work alone. I rely on Mikellides and any available empirical evidence to ground my theoretical framework.

### Opening Statement: Judith Heerwagen — Biophilic Design and the Evidence for Natural Material Benefits

I bring the design-specification bridge. My work with GSA (U.S. General Services Administration) on biophilic design in federal buildings demonstrated that offices with natural materials (wood finishes, stone surfaces, natural fibre textiles) combined with daylight and nature views showed 6–12% improvement in self-reported well-being, 8–11% reduction in sick leave, and measurable cognitive performance gains on standardized tasks (Heerwagen & Hase, 2001; Heerwagen, 2009). These are real-world field studies, not laboratory experiments.

For NATURAL_MATERIAL_CONVERGENCE_001, my data provide the architectural translation layer: how do Li's phytoncide concentrations map to building specifications? Unfinished or lightly finished wood surfaces in ventilated offices produce phytoncide concentrations of ~20–80 μg/m³ — lower than forest levels but sufficient for measurable cortisol reduction (~8% vs. Li's ~15% in forest conditions). The dose-response is sublinear: doubling the wood surface area does not double the stress reduction. The convergence bonus (visual + haptic + olfactory) exceeds the sum of individual channels by approximately 15–25%, consistent with multisensory facilitation.

## CRUCIBLE DEBATES

### Debate 1: CT Afferent Architectural Translation — Can Lab Data Cross to Railings?

**Morrison**: My data are from controlled forearm stroking at 1–10 cm/s. Architectural touch is fundamentally different: people grip railings (palm contact, static or slow slide), walk on floors (sole contact, varying pressure), lean against walls (shoulder/back contact, sustained static). CT afferents are found primarily in hairy skin, and their density varies enormously by body region. I cannot certify that the forearm CT firing curves translate to palmar gripping or plantar contact.

**McGlone**: I can partially bridge this gap. My laboratory has measured CT-like responses in glabrous skin of the palm (Watkins et al., 2021) — lower density than hairy skin, but present. The hedonic ratings from palmar contact with wood vs. metal vs. stone follow the same rank ordering as forearm stroking data, though the magnitudes are smaller (~60% of the forearm hedonic response for equivalent surface properties).

**Morrison**: That 60% scaling factor is crucial. It means architectural haptic parameters should be discounted relative to laboratory data. And for feet (sole contact with floors), we have almost no CT firing data. The sole of the foot has very low CT density; floor material effects may operate primarily through temperature and compliance rather than CT afferents per se.

**Spence**: The crossmodal framework offers a workaround: even if direct CT activation from architectural surfaces is attenuated relative to laboratory forearm stroking, the VISUAL expectation of pleasant touch can produce anticipatory affect. Seeing wood (warm, natural visual texture) generates an expectation of pleasant touch via crossmodal correspondence. This visual-haptic expectation effect operates even without actual contact.

**Panel consensus**: CT_AFFECTIVE_TOUCH_001 uses Morrison's forearm data as the reference but applies a body-site attenuation factor: palm = 0.60 × forearm; fingertips = 0.55 × forearm; sole = 0.30 × forearm (primarily thermal/compliance rather than CT). All architectural CT parameters carry CAPACITY (0.45) maximum warrant as specified by the pre-panel review. The visual expectation pathway (Spence) is a separate mechanism with its own confidence (0.50, EMPIRICAL_COVARIANCE).

### Debate 2: Convergence Bonus — Super-Additive, Additive, or Sub-Additive?

**Rowland**: The Stein & Meredith framework predicts super-additivity under conditions of inverse effectiveness — when individual channels are weak. For natural materials at full signal strength (well-lit room, direct touch, detectable scent), the prediction is additivity or mild sub-additivity. Super-additivity is the exception, not the rule, when unisensory signals are strong.

**Heerwagen**: My field data suggest a convergence bonus of approximately 15–25% above the sum of individual channel effects. This is super-additive by definition. But I measured OUTCOMES (well-being, sick leave) rather than NEURAL responses. The discrepancy might be because real-world environments never have all channels at full signal strength — there is always some degradation, activating inverse effectiveness.

**Li**: My phytoncide data are single-channel (olfactory isolation in controlled chambers). I have not measured multi-channel convergence. But the physiological pathways converge: visual calming (parasympathetic via PPA → ventral visual → amygdala modulation) + haptic calming (CT → insular → parasympathetic) + olfactory calming (phytoncide → olfactory bulb → limbic → HPA axis suppression) all terminate in parasympathetic dominance. Convergent endpoints from independent pathways predicts super-additivity at the physiological level.

**Ernst**: The MLE framework predicts precision improvement, not magnitude amplification. Multi-channel integration makes the material percept MORE PRECISE (lower variance), but does not necessarily increase the hedonic intensity. Heerwagen's field data might show super-additive well-being because multiple channels provide redundancy (robustness) rather than because each channel multiplicatively amplifies the others.

**Panel consensus**: NATURAL_MATERIAL_CONVERGENCE_001 uses a convergence bonus of 1.20 (20% above sum of individual channels) as the default, following Heerwagen's field data. The mechanism is specified as convergent parasympathetic activation from independent pathways (Li's interpretation) rather than neural super-additivity (Rowland notes super-additivity requires weak individual signals). Confidence on the convergence bonus: 0.45 (CAPACITY) — field data support the direction and approximate magnitude, but the mechanism-level justification requires controlled multi-channel parametric studies that do not yet exist.

### Debate 3: Bayesian Material Integration — Laboratory to Architecture

**Ernst**: My MLE model predicts that visual-haptic integration improves material roughness discrimination by ~30–40% (Ernst & Banks, 2002). But the model assumes: (a) independent noise in each channel; (b) common cause (the brain infers that visual and haptic signals come from the same object); (c) Gaussian likelihood functions. In a laboratory, these assumptions hold. In an architectural environment, (a) may fail — visual and haptic channels may share common noise sources (vibration, lighting flicker); (b) is usually satisfied for material perception (you see and touch the same wall); (c) is an approximation.

**Spence**: I would add that real architectural materials are perceived in CONTEXT. A wood surface in a warm, well-lit room is perceived differently from the same wood surface in a cold, dim basement. The context modulates the prior probability in Ernst's Bayesian framework, biasing the integrated percept toward the expected material properties. This is why material perception is not just a bottom-up integration problem — it is a top-down, expectation-modulated Bayesian inference.

**Ernst**: Agreed. For MATERIAL_IDENTITY_INTEGRATION_001, I propose that the MLE precision gain (30–40% improvement in discrimination) is a laboratory estimate. The architectural translation should assume a conservative 20–30% precision gain for multi-modal material experience (seeing + touching), reflecting the additional noise and contextual modulation in real environments. Bridge warrant: FUNCTIONAL (0.50) as per the constraint.

**Panel consensus**: MATERIAL_IDENTITY_INTEGRATION_001 uses MLE precision gain of 25% (midpoint of 20–30% architectural estimate) for visual-haptic integration. Adding olfactory channel (natural materials) contributes an additional ~10% precision gain (weaker channel, lower reliability weight). Total multi-modal precision gain: ~35% for visual + haptic + olfactory vs. visual alone. Bridge warrant: FUNCTIONAL (0.50) for the integration model; EMPIRICAL_COVARIANCE for the visual-haptic component; CAPACITY for the olfactory addition.

### Debate 4: Cultural Conditioning — Can Any Parameter Be Universal?

**Mikellides**: I must be emphatic: material evaluative associations are culturally constructed. My studies across Mediterranean, Northern European, and East Asian contexts show systematic differences for every material tested. The rank ordering is NOT consistent across cultures for concrete, marble, steel, or glass. Only WOOD shows a nearly universal positive evaluation across all studied cultures — and even for wood, the DEGREE of positive evaluation varies (highest in Nordic and Japanese contexts, moderate in Mediterranean).

**Pallasmaa**: I would argue that the universal appeal of wood is not cultural but biological — it reflects evolutionary familiarity with natural materials. The biophilic hypothesis (Wilson, 1984) predicts that materials encountered during human evolution (wood, stone, water, earth) should produce more positive affect than evolutionarily novel materials (steel, glass, plastic, concrete), independent of culture.

**Li**: My phytoncide data support Pallasmaa on wood specifically. The physiological stress reduction from wood VOCs operates below conscious awareness and is consistent across Japanese and European participant samples. The olfactory pathway is likely innate.

**Mikellides**: I accept that wood may be a partial exception — biologically grounded positive evaluation modulated by cultural amplification. But for MATERIAL_CULTURAL_CONDITIONING_001, the template must specify: innate biophilic baseline (applicable across populations) + cultural modifier (population-specific). And the cultural modifier can REVERSE the innate baseline for some materials — concrete is biophilically neutral or mildly aversive, but Tadao Andō's concrete is positively evaluated in Japanese architectural culture.

**Panel consensus**: MATERIAL_CULTURAL_CONDITIONING_001 uses a two-layer model: (a) biophilic baseline (wood positive, natural stone mildly positive, processed materials neutral, synthetic materials mildly negative) at confidence 0.45 (CAPACITY); (b) cultural modifier (population-specific, additive, can reverse the baseline) at confidence 0.40 (THEORETICAL_DEFAULT). The template specifies four cultural reference populations: Western-European, Nordic, East Asian (Japanese), Mediterranean. Other populations require explicit parameter estimation before use.

### Debate 5: Patina and Temporal Depth — Phenomenology Meets Evidence

**Pallasmaa**: A new building communicates nothing except the intentions of its makers. A building that has aged — whose stone has weathered, whose wood has worn, whose bronze has patinated — communicates the passage of time and the accumulation of human experience. This temporal depth is perceptually available: we SEE the wear pattern, we FEEL the smoothed threshold, we SMELL the aged wood. It produces what I call an "existential anchoring" — a sense that one is situated within a continuum of human habitation rather than in an ahistorical void.

**Mikellides**: Juhani, I respect your framework deeply, but we need empirical grounding. I can offer some: my studies show that materials with visible patina and wear are rated as more "authentic," "characterful," and "trustworthy" than pristine equivalents, with effect sizes of d ≈ 0.40–0.60 on semantic differential scales (Mikellides, 1990, supplementary data on aged vs. new stone facades). The effect is robust in European contexts but may not generalize — newly built traditional architecture in the Gulf states, for instance, is valued precisely FOR its pristine, unworn quality.

**Heerwagen**: I can add that post-occupancy evaluations of buildings with natural material aging features (exposed timber that darkens, copper cladding that patinates, stone that weathers) show higher satisfaction ratings at 5 and 10 years than buildings with static finishes (painted surfaces, anodized metal, sealed stone). The satisfaction trajectory is UPWARD for aging materials, FLAT or DOWNWARD for static materials. This is suggestive of temporal depth value but confounded with novelty effects.

**Panel consensus**: MATERIAL_AGING_TEMPORAL_DEPTH_001 calibrates two parameters: (a) authenticity/character rating boost (d ≈ 0.40–0.60) for visibly aged vs. pristine materials, from Mikellides' data, at confidence 0.45 (CAPACITY); (b) satisfaction trajectory benefit for aging vs. static materials over occupancy duration, from Heerwagen's field observations, at confidence 0.40 (THEORETICAL_DEFAULT). Pallasmaa's "existential anchoring" concept provides the theoretical framework but does not independently support any specific parameter value.

## CMR INTEGRATION NOTE

### How MULTI-I Templates Compose

The nine MULTI-I templates form a system organised around three functional layers:

**Layer 1 — Sensory Mechanism** (how the brain processes architectural materials):
MATERIAL_IDENTITY_INTEGRATION_001 (Bayesian multi-modal integration), CT_AFFECTIVE_TOUCH_001 (affective touch pathway), MSI_INVERSE_EFFECTIVENESS_002 (degradation compensation), MSI_CONGRUENCY_PRINCIPLE_001 (congruency → fluency), CROSSMODAL_CONGRUENCE_001 (spatial coherence).

**Layer 2 — Material Affect** (how materials produce emotional responses):
HAP_SURFACE_MATERIAL_001 (material-specific hedonic profiles), NATURAL_MATERIAL_CONVERGENCE_001 (multi-channel stress reduction from natural materials).

**Layer 3 — Learned Evaluation** (how culture and time modulate material perception):
MATERIAL_CULTURAL_CONDITIONING_001 (cultural associations), MATERIAL_AGING_TEMPORAL_DEPTH_001 (patina as perceptual enrichment).

The builder AI computes material affect as: (Layer 1 sensory precision × Layer 2 material hedonic profile) + Layer 3 cultural modifier, with congruency and coherence as gating conditions (incongruent or incoherent environments produce negative affect regardless of material quality).

### Key Cross-Panel Dependencies

**VISUAL-I T1** → NATURAL_MATERIAL_CONVERGENCE_001: Visual fractal channel is VISUAL-I's; convergence bonus is MULTI-I's. No double-counting.

**STRESS-I T6/T7** → NATURAL_MATERIAL_CONVERGENCE_001: Natural material stress reduction antagonises the cortisol pathway (T6) and supports body budget restoration (T7). The builder AI should compute: T6 cortisol level × (1 - NATURAL_MATERIAL_CONVERGENCE_001 reduction factor).

**MEMORY-I** → MSI_CONGRUENCY_PRINCIPLE_001: Congruent environments are encoded more strongly (processing fluency → better hippocampal encoding). The congruency affect boost feeds into ED_HIPPOCAMPAL_ENCODING_001's encoding strength parameter.

### Partial-Out Protocols

**CT and HAP**: CT_AFFECTIVE_TOUCH_001 provides the neural mechanism; HAP_SURFACE_MATERIAL_001 provides the material-specific parameters. These are not additive — they are the same pathway parameterised at different levels. The builder AI uses HAP hedonic scores (which are CT-mediated) and does not add a separate CT bonus on top.

**Congruency and Coherence**: MSI_CONGRUENCY_PRINCIPLE_001 (channel-level congruency) and CROSSMODAL_CONGRUENCE_001 (spatial coherence) are related but distinct. Channel congruency asks "do the channels agree on material identity?" Spatial coherence asks "do the channels agree on spatial location?" Both can be satisfied or violated independently. The builder AI computes both and takes the minimum as the gating score.

**Cultural and Biophilic**: MATERIAL_CULTURAL_CONDITIONING_001's biophilic baseline overlaps with NATURAL_MATERIAL_CONVERGENCE_001's stress reduction pathway. The biophilic baseline is the EVALUATIVE response (positive affect from evolutionary familiarity); the natural material convergence is the PHYSIOLOGICAL response (parasympathetic activation from phytoncides, CT, and fractal statistics). These are complementary, not redundant, and should be computed independently.
