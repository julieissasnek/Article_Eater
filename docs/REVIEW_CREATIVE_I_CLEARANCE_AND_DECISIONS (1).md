## A-2. Structural Issues

### ISSUE 1: Template Count Mismatch (MUST RESOLVE)

The GAP_PANEL_MASTER_PLAN specifies **5 templates** for CREATIVE-I. This
pre-panel document lists **7 templates**. The discrepancy arises from two
CROSS_ variant templates:

- CROSS_CREATIVE_NETWORK_DYNAMICS_001
- CROSS_ENVIRONMENTAL_PROCESSING_STYLE_001

These may be legitimate expansions generated during gap-stub elaboration, but
the pre-panel document does not explain where the extra two came from or cite
the GAP_PANEL_MASTER_PLAN entry that authorises them. The CROSS_ templates
each have relatively few missing parameters (1 and 2 respectively), which
suggests they may be thin extensions of their base templates rather than
genuinely independent calibration targets.

**Decision**: Accept 7 templates but add an explicit scope-justification note.
The CROSS_ templates are defensible — they own environmental modulation and
cross-modal interaction, which are distinct from the base neural mechanisms.
But Cowork must confirm in the panel output that each CROSS_ template produces
a mechanism chain that is NOT a subset of its base template. If during panel
execution the Crucible finds that a CROSS_ template adds no independent
mechanism steps, it should be folded into its base template and the count
reduced to 6. This is a soft ceiling, not a hard one.

### ISSUE 2: Missing "Insight / Aha Moment" Template

The GAP_PANEL_MASTER_PLAN lists five expected CREATIVE-I topics:

1. Creative flow / Csikszentmihalyi state
2. Divergent thinking environmental modulators
3. Incubation / mind-wandering spatial facilitators
4. **Insight / Aha moment architectural triggers**
5. Creative collaboration spatial dynamics

Topic 4 appears to have been absorbed or dropped. The pre-panel document does
not contain a dedicated insight/aha template. INCUBATION_ARCHITECTURE_001
covers incubation but not the distinct insight phase — and these are different
constructs in the literature (Sio & Ormerod, 2009, distinguish incubation
effects from insight problem-solving; Kounios & Beeman, 2014, identify distinct
neural signatures for aha moments involving anterior superior temporal gyrus
gamma bursts that are mechanistically separable from DMN incubation dynamics).

**Decision**: Flag for Cowork. The panel should either (a) include an explicit
insight/aha mechanism step within CREATIVE_NETWORK_DYNAMICS_001 (the DMN-ECN
coupling template, where the aha moment represents the transition from DMN
incubation to ECN-mediated conscious insight), or (b) explain in the Output
Block 4 residual-gaps section why insight was excluded and schedule it for
CROSSCUT-I. Option (a) is preferred — the Kounios & Beeman (2014) gamma-burst
finding maps naturally onto the DMN→ECN handoff that the network dynamics
template already models.

### ISSUE 3: No Crucible Debate Pairings Specified

Prior pre-panel clearances (MUSIC-I, THERMAL-I) specified explicit Crucible
debate pairings — the contested theoretical positions that the panel must
adjudicate. This document identifies evidence quality flags (§4) and scope
ambiguities (§3.2) but does not translate them into formal debate pairings.

**Decision**: Add the following Crucible debate pairings to the clearance:

- **Debate 1**: DMN re-engagement mechanism — Is DMN-ECN coupling for creativity
  a graded/continuous process (Beaty et al., 2016; functional connectivity
  view) or a discrete phase-transition (Christoff et al., 2016; dynamic
  systems view)? This matters because graded coupling implies linear
  environmental dose-response, while phase-transition implies threshold effects.

- **Debate 2**: Walking-creativity pathway — Motor-afferent → divergent thinking
  (Oppezzo & Schwartz, 2014; embodied route via proprioceptive stimulation)
  vs. attentional-release → mind-wandering → divergent thinking (Baird et al.,
  2012; cognitive route via reduced executive control). These produce different
  architectural implications: the motor route favours corridors and stairs; the
  cognitive route favours nature views and soft fascination.

- **Debate 3**: Environmental stimulation for creativity — Inverted-U / Yerkes-
  Dodson (moderate stimulation optimal; Mehta et al., 2012, on ambient noise)
  vs. differential-mode model (low stimulation for divergent, high stimulation
  for convergent; cognitive load theory). This is the theoretical backbone of
  PROCESSING_STYLE_MODULATION_001.

### ISSUE 5: IE-DPT Framing Not Mentioned

The transfer document (§3) is explicit: "All panels since SOCIAL-I use IE-DPT
framing in their CMR Integration Notes (OUTPUT BLOCK 3)." The pre-panel
document makes no reference to IE-DPT (Implicit-Explicit Dimensional Processing
Theory) anywhere. This is a standing-orders compliance gap.

Creativity is an excellent domain for IE-DPT application. The implicit/explicit
dimension maps cleanly onto the divergent/convergent distinction:

- **Implicit processing** → DMN-dominated incubation, mind-wandering, associative
  spreading activation (divergent thinking)
- **Explicit processing** → ECN-dominated evaluation, constraint satisfaction,
  convergent selection

The environmental Goldilocks zone for creativity likely has different optima
along the implicit/explicit axis — low-stimulation environments for implicit
creative incubation, moderate-to-structured environments for explicit creative
evaluation.

**Decision**: Add to constraints:

> **C-08**: All templates must include IE-DPT framing in mechanism chains.
> The implicit/explicit dimension maps onto divergent (implicit-dominant) vs.
> convergent (explicit-dominant) processing. Output Block 3 (CMR Integration
> Notes) must specify where each template's primary mechanism falls on the
> IE-DPT axis and identify any implicit→explicit transitions.

### ISSUE 6: Barrett-Craig Dependency Not Addressed

INCUBATION_ARCHITECTURE_001 depends on low arousal / parasympathetic dominance
as a precondition for creative incubation. This implicates interoceptive
processing — and specifically the Barrett-Craig debate resolved in THERMAL-I.
If the two-stage compromise is adopted (see PART B, Decision 1 below), the
incubation template should specify which stage is relevant: Stage 1 (posterior
insula, sensory) or Stage 2 (anterior insula, evaluative/constructionist).

The answer is almost certainly Stage 2: the evaluative appraisal of bodily
state as "safe/rested/available for mind-wandering" is a constructionist
judgment, not a labelled-line sensory event. This connects incubation to
Barrett's (2017) body-budget framework — the body budget must be in surplus for
the DMN to be released from homeostatic monitoring into unconstrained
associative processing.

**Decision**: Add to constraints:

> **C-09**: INCUBATION_ARCHITECTURE_001 must specify its interoceptive
> prerequisite using the Barrett-Craig two-stage model (if adopted — see
> Decision 1). The relevant stage is Stage 2 (anterior insula, evaluative/
> constructionist). The mechanism is: body-budget surplus → reduced
> interoceptive prediction error → DMN release from homeostatic monitoring →
> incubation-available state.

## Decision 1: Barrett-Craig Two-Stage Compromise — Adopt as CMR Standard Model?

### The Proposal

THERMAL-I resolved the Barrett–Craig debate via a two-stage anatomical
compromise:

- **Stage 1** (posterior insula): sensory, modality-specific thermoreception —
  Craig's (2002, 2009) labelled-line model prevails
- **Stage 2** (anterior insula): evaluative, constructionist appraisal —
  Barrett's (2017) body-budget / constructed-emotion model prevails

The question is whether to adopt this as the CMR-wide standard model for all
interoceptive processing, or leave each panel to derive its own model.

### Cost-Benefit

**Costs of adopting:**

- Creates a load-bearing assumption. If the two-stage model is wrong or
  substantially revised (e.g., Khalsa et al., 2018, suggest a more distributed
  interoceptive network than a simple two-stage gradient), every panel that
  inherits it needs revision.
- Propagation scope is wide: STRESS-I (T7 body budget), NEUROMOD-I (HPA
  cascade, allostatic integration), CREATIVE-I (incubation prerequisites),
  and CROSSCUT-I all have interoceptive dependencies.
- The model conflates a neuroanatomical gradient (posterior→anterior insula)
  with a processing distinction (sensory→evaluative). The gradient is well-
  documented (Kurth et al., 2010; Craig, 2009); the clean separation into
  two discrete stages is a useful idealisation but may overstate the sharpness
  of the boundary.

**Benefits of adopting:**

- Prevents each panel from independently re-debating the same Barrett vs.
  Craig question — a waste of Crucible time and a source of inconsistency.
- Provides a shared vocabulary for interoceptive mechanism chains across panels.
- The compromise is well-supported: the posterior-to-anterior insular gradient
  is one of the more robust findings in human neuroimaging (Kurth et al., 2010,
  meta-analysis of 1,768 experiments; Chang et al., 2013, whole-brain parcellation).
- Six templates across four panels already implicitly assume something like
  this model. Formalising it makes the assumption auditable.

**Costs of NOT adopting:**

- Each panel derives its own interoceptive model. STRESS-I might lean Barrett,
  NEUROMOD-I might lean Craig, CREATIVE-I might split the difference. This
  produces incoherent mechanism chains when templates are compared across panels.
- Cross-template flags that reference interoception become harder to resolve.
- The Crucible wastes time re-litigating settled territory.

### Recommendation: ADOPT — with revision clause

Adopt the two-stage compromise as a CMR working model with the following
safeguards:

1. **Label it explicitly as a working model**, not a settled fact. Every
   template that uses it should include a qualifier: "Two-stage insular model
   (CMR working model; see THERMAL-I Barrett-Craig resolution)."

2. **Add a revision trigger**: If any future panel's Crucible produces evidence
   that the two-stage model is inadequate for its domain (e.g., distributed
   interoceptive processing in social cognition), that panel may propose a
   local amendment, which is then reviewed for cross-panel consistency.

3. **Issue a cross-panel position statement** (brief, 1 page) documenting the
   model and its boundary conditions, to be appended to the Architecture
   Explanation document.

**Status: DECIDED — Adopt with revision clause. Action items: cross-panel
position statement (assign to Cowork or next Opus/Chat session); add working-
model qualifier to all templates using the two-stage model.**

## Decision 2: AX4 (Perceived Control) — Formal Elevation via Option B?

### The Proposal

AX4 (Perceived Control) has been validated as a cross-cutting moderator across
17+ templates with a consistent effect size of d ≈ 0.45. The question is
whether to formally elevate it via "Option B" — presumably promoting it from an
ad-hoc interaction coefficient to a structurally recognised cross-cutting
moderator with its own entry in the tier architecture.

### Cost-Benefit

**Costs of elevating:**

- Adds structural complexity to the tier architecture. AX4 would be the first
  formally elevated cross-cutting moderator, establishing a precedent. Future
  candidates (e.g., cultural conditioning, age/development) would expect the
  same treatment.
- The structural repair sprint (Waves 0–4) has progressed past Wave 2.
  E-01 (canonical schema) and M-01 (migration) are complete or in progress,
  so the schema infrastructure can now accommodate AX4's new structural role
  without the coordination burden that would have existed earlier.
- The d ≈ 0.45 estimate comes primarily from Schweiker & Wagner (2015) for
  thermal comfort. "Comparable across domains" is asserted in the transfer
  document but not backed by a formal cross-domain meta-analysis. The true
  cross-domain effect size might vary substantially (perceived control over
  lighting ≠ perceived control over social density ≠ perceived control over
  acoustic environment).

**Benefits of elevating:**

- Formalises what is already functioning as a cross-cutting moderator. AX4 is
  already referenced in 17+ templates; leaving it informal means each template
  re-implements it slightly differently.
- A formal entry provides a single point of calibration — when better data
  arrives, one update propagates everywhere.
- The evidence base is genuinely strong: perceived control moderates stress
  responses (Steptoe & Appels, 1989), thermal comfort (Schweiker & Wagner,
  2015; de Dear & Brager, 1998), acoustic satisfaction (Frontczak et al.,
  2012), and workplace wellbeing (Vischer, 2007). The construct validity
  across domains is well-established even if the precise effect size varies.

**Costs of NOT elevating:**

- AX4 continues to exist informally in 17+ templates with no canonical
  definition. Drift and inconsistency accumulate.
- The variable ontology crisis (931 roots, 1,500 with affixes) gets worse
  rather than better.

### Recommendation: ELEVATE — implementation triggered now (Wave 2 complete)

The evidential case for elevation is strong, and the repair sprint has now
passed Wave 2, so the timing concern is resolved.

1. **Formally decide now**: AX4 is a recognised cross-cutting moderator in
   the CMR architecture. Record this in the transfer document and architecture
   explanation.

2. **Assign structural implementation to CC immediately** (current repair
   sprint wave). The implementation task is: define AX4's canonical schema
   entry, specify its interaction with each T1 framework, and add it to the
   ceiling lint tool. Wave 2 (E-01 canonical schema, M-01 migration) is
   complete, so the schema infrastructure exists to accommodate AX4.

3. **Allow domain-specific effect sizes**: Do not impose a single d = 0.45
   across all domains. Instead, treat 0.45 as the default prior and allow each
   panel to calibrate its own domain-specific AX4 magnitude (e.g., AX4_thermal
   = 0.45, AX4_acoustic = 0.35, AX4_lighting = 0.50).

**Status: DECIDED — Elevate. Wave 2 is complete; structural implementation
is TRIGGERED NOW. Assign to CC repair sprint (current wave). Allow domain-
specific calibration.**

## Decision 3: Aesthetic Anchoring — Promote from Candidate to Formal T1.5?

### The Proposal

Aesthetic Anchoring (PP + SN parentage) was nominated as a T1.5 candidate
during a Session 8 T22 debate. The question is whether to promote it to a
formal T1.5 theory alongside the existing 10.

### Cost-Benefit

**Costs of promoting now:**

- The evidence trail in the transfer document is thin: "Session 8 T22 debate"
  with no Crucible summary, no calibrated effect sizes, no competing accounts,
  no inline Toulmin. The other 10 T1.5 theories each have reduction documents,
  parent T1 mappings, and (for the later ones) formal specification.
- Promoting on thin evidence sets a bad precedent — the entire point of the
  tiered architecture is that promotion requires evidential warrant.
- The construct itself is somewhat unclear. "Aesthetic Anchoring" could mean
  several things: first-impression anchoring bias applied to environmental
  aesthetics (Kahneman & Tversky tradition), aesthetic fluency as a processing
  heuristic (Reber et al., 2004), or initial affective response setting a
  context for subsequent evaluation (Barrett's affective realism). Each of
  these implies a different mechanism chain.

**Benefits of promoting now:**

- Gets it into the formal roster, which means CROSSCUT-I (S-08) can calibrate
  it properly rather than treating it as an orphan.
- PP + SN is a plausible parent pair — predictive processing provides the
  expectation/prediction-error mechanism, salience network provides the
  relevance detection.

**Costs of NOT promoting now:**

- Minimal. It remains a candidate, which is a legitimate status. CROSSCUT-I
  can still address it.

### Recommendation: DO NOT PROMOTE — keep as candidate, schedule for CROSSCUT-I

The evidence base does not currently support promotion. The right path is:

1. **Keep Aesthetic Anchoring as a candidate T1.5** (its current status).

2. **Add it to the CROSSCUT-I panel scope** as a formal evaluation target.
   CROSSCUT-I (S-08, 15 templates) is explicitly designed for meta-analytic
   reconciliation — this is exactly where candidate theories should be tested.

3. **Before CROSSCUT-I**, produce a brief reduction document (comparable to the
   T1.5 Expansion documents for Biophilia, Prospect-Refuge, etc.) that
   specifies: (a) the construct definition, (b) parent T1 mapping with
   mechanism chains, (c) candidate bridge warrant type and ceiling, and (d)
   competing accounts. This can be assigned to Cowork as a pre-panel task.

**Status: DEFERRED — remains candidate. Scheduled for CROSSCUT-I evaluation.
Pre-panel reduction document to be produced before S-08.**
