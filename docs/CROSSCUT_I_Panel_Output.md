# ROUND TABLE — Opening Statements

## Berman (Meta-Analyst — Dose-Response)

The central challenge for this panel is that dose-response relationships in environmental psychology are neither universal nor arbitrarily domain-specific — they occupy a structured middle ground. My work on nature exposure (Berman, Jonides & Kaplan, 2008; Berman et al., 2012, N=38) shows a log-linear dose-response for cognitive restoration: the first 20 minutes of nature exposure produce the largest restoration benefit; subsequent exposure continues to help but with diminishing returns. Barton & Pretty (2010, meta-analysis of 1,252 participants across 10 UK studies) confirmed this for green exercise: the first 5 minutes produced the greatest mood and self-esteem improvement (d = 0.60 for first 5 minutes vs. d = 0.30 for subsequent 30 minutes). These are log-linear or power-law curves, not linear or sigmoid.

But thermal comfort follows a different functional form entirely — an inverted U (de Dear & Brager, 2002) centred on the adaptive comfort temperature, where deviation in either direction produces discomfort. Noise dose-response is yet another shape: roughly linear in the low-to-moderate range (Basner et al., 2014, meta-analysis of 22 studies, N > 14,000), with acceleration above 65 dB. The CMR must accommodate multiple functional forms, each empirically grounded, rather than imposing a single universal curve.

## Friston (Computational Neuroscience — Predictive Processing)

The dose-response diversity that Marc describes is precisely what we would expect from a precision-weighting framework. The brain does not process all environmental parameters with the same functional form because the precision structure of each parameter differs. Nature exposure follows a log-linear curve because the brain's uncertainty about the natural environment decreases rapidly with initial exposure (high information gain in the first minutes) and then asymptotes. Thermal deviation follows an inverted U because the brain has a narrow prior centred on homeostatic temperature — deviations in either direction increase free energy. Noise follows an approximately linear dose-response at low-moderate levels because the auditory system's dynamic range compresses signal linearly in that regime.

The unifying principle is free-energy minimisation: the dose-response function for each environmental parameter is determined by the shape of the brain's prior over that parameter and the precision (inverse variance) of the incoming sensory evidence. This is not a universal curve but a universal generative principle that produces domain-specific curves.

However — and I want to be clear about this constraint — the free-energy framework is a computational account that provides the organising logic. The specific functional forms must be empirically determined for each domain. The framework predicts the general shape (log-linear for high-entropy parameters, inverted-U for homeostatic parameters) but not the specific slopes or inflection points.

## DeYoung (Individual Differences)

The cybernetic Big Five theory (DeYoung, 2015) provides a neuroscience-grounded framework for individual differences in environmental sensitivity. Each Big Five dimension maps onto a biological system that modulates environmental processing:

- **Neuroticism** maps onto threat sensitivity (amygdala-HPA axis, NM8 from NEUROMOD-I). High neuroticism amplifies response to environmental stressors: noise, crowding, spatial threat cues. The environmental dose-response curve shifts LEFT for high-neuroticism individuals — they reach discomfort at lower doses.
- **Extraversion** maps onto reward sensitivity (mesolimbic DA, NM1-NM4). High extraversion amplifies response to environmental rewards: novelty, social stimulation, sensory richness. The dose-response curve shifts RIGHT — they need higher stimulation to reach optimal arousal.
- **Openness to Experience** maps onto cognitive exploration (DMN-ECN coupling, CREATIVE-I mechanisms). High openness amplifies engagement with complex, novel environments. They benefit more from architectural complexity and creative spatial configuration.

But I must add an essential caveat that the clearance review correctly flags: the Big Five are not the only relevant individual differences. Sensory processing sensitivity (Aron & Aron, 1997; estimated 15-20% prevalence) is not well-captured by neuroticism alone — it involves heightened processing across ALL sensory modalities, not just threat. ADHD-related variance in optimal NE levels (Arnsten, 2006) is not captured by any Big Five dimension. These neurodiversity dimensions must be structurally present in AX_INDIVIDUAL_DIFFERENCES_008 even if we can only provide THEORETICAL_DEFAULT calibration.

## Han (Cultural Neuroscience)

Cultural modulation of environmental response is both more pervasive and more specific than typically assumed. Our work (Han & Northoff, 2008; Han et al., 2013) shows that culture modulates neural processing at the level of basic perceptual encoding, not just higher-order evaluation. East Asian perceivers show greater contextual processing (embedding the focal object in its background), while Western perceivers show greater focal processing (extracting the object from its context). In architectural terms: the same building is literally perceived differently by individuals from different cultural backgrounds — East Asian visitors may attend more to how the building sits in its landscape context, while Western visitors may attend more to the building as a standalone object.

For the CMR, cultural modulation operates at multiple levels: (a) perceptual encoding of spatial features (analytic vs. holistic processing), (b) aesthetic evaluation (feng shui principles, wabi-sabi, biophilic preferences vary by culture), (c) social-spatial norms (personal space distances, crowding thresholds, privacy expectations), and (d) thermal and lighting preferences (adaptive comfort standards vary by climate culture). AX_CULTURAL_MODULATION_009 should specify a general cultural-moderation framework with domain-specific calibration rather than a single cultural discount factor.

## Schooler (Ecological Rationality)

The ecological rationality framework (Gigerenzer, Todd & the ABC Research Group, 1999) proposes that organisms use simple, context-specific heuristics that exploit the information structure of their environment rather than optimal decision algorithms. In architectural contexts, this means occupants do not perform utility calculations when choosing routes, workspaces, or comfort settings — they use fast and frugal heuristics shaped by the environment's informational structure.

The most architecturally relevant heuristic is the recognition heuristic (Goldstein & Gigerenzer, 2002): when choosing between two options, if one is recognised and the other is not, infer the recognised one has higher value on the criterion. In wayfinding, this translates to: follow familiar paths, approach recognised landmarks, avoid unfamiliar corridors. The fluency heuristic (Schooler & Hertwig, 2005) extends this: more fluently processed environments (legible, coherent spatial structure) are preferred. This provides an ecological-rationality grounding for the spatial legibility findings from SPATIAL-I.

Whether this yields an EMPIRICAL_COVARIANCE bridge to architectural behaviour remains to be demonstrated. I am aware of the C-11 constraint (achieve at least one EMPIRICAL_COVARIANCE step or downgrade to stub). The fluency heuristic has been tested in recognition memory and preference paradigms (Schooler & Hertwig, 2005, N=60, d = 0.45 for fluency → preference) but not in architectural navigation. I will work to establish the bridge, but I want to be transparent about the inferential distance.

## Keltner (Awe Phenomenology)

Awe is the emotion of perceived vastness and need for accommodation (Keltner & Haidt, 2003). It is the architectural emotion par excellence — cathedrals, grand vistas, monumental public spaces, and sublime landscapes all trigger awe. Our research (Keltner & Piff, 2014; Piff et al., 2015, N = 1,500 across 5 studies) shows that awe produces a distinctive phenomenological profile: (a) perceived vastness — the stimulus exceeds the observer's current conceptual framework; (b) small-self — diminished self-focus, reduced sense of personal importance; (c) prosociality — increased generosity, cooperation, and collective identification.

The neural mechanisms are becoming clearer. Yaden et al. (2019, systematic review) identify DMN suppression (consistent with small-self), increased anterior insula activation (consistent with interoceptive surprise per Barrett-Craig), and SN engagement (consistent with salience detection of the vast stimulus). Van Elk et al. (2019, N=32, VR-induced awe via virtual cathedral) showed increased skin conductance, reduced self-referential processing in PCC (DMN hub), and increased anterior insula activation — d = 0.55 for DMN suppression, d = 0.40 for anterior insula increase.

For the CMR, awe provides the mechanism by which monumental architecture produces its distinctive psychological effects. The consolidation of HIGH_PE and NEED_FOR_ACCOMMODATION into AX3_AWE_MECHANISM_001 is correct — the prediction error IS the need for accommodation, described in PP terms. AX3_SMALL_SELF_001 captures the downstream consequence that makes awe architecturally significant: the shift from self-focused to collective-focused processing that monumental spaces induce.

## Uddin (Network Neuroscience — Salience Network)

The triple-network model (Menon, 2011; Uddin, 2015) provides the computational architecture for understanding how environmental stimuli are routed between processing modes. The salience network (anterior insula + dorsal anterior cingulate) detects behaviourally relevant stimuli and switches the brain between the default mode network (internal, self-referential processing) and the task-positive network (external, goal-directed processing). This switching mechanism was validated in CREATIVE-I (Crucible 1 consensus: SN-mediated switching model adopted for DMN-ECN coupling).

CROSSCUT-I's task is to generalise this beyond creativity: SN switching governs how the brain responds to ALL environmental stimuli that cross a salience threshold. A novel spatial configuration (architectural novelty → SN detection → DMN→TPN switch → focused attention on the new feature), a social encounter (unexpected person → SN detection → social processing), an alarm signal (fire alarm → SN detection → threat processing). The SALIENCE_NETWORK_SWITCH_001 template should specify the general switching dynamics (latency, threshold, recovery time) that apply across all domain-specific templates.

The key parameter is switching latency — estimated at 200-400ms from SN detection to DMN→TPN mode change (Sridharan et al., 2008, N=18, fMRI with ms-resolution EEG). This is fast enough to be automatic (IE-DPT Level 1) and sets a temporal floor for how quickly occupants can re-orient attention to environmental changes.

## Saalmann (Thalamic Gating)

The thalamic reticular nucleus (TRN) is the brain's gatekeeper for sensory information. It receives input from all cortical areas and provides inhibitory feedback to thalamic relay nuclei, selectively gating which sensory channels reach cortex (Crick, 1984; Pinault, 2004). This is the earliest stage at which the brain filters environmental input — before cortical prediction errors, before precision weighting, before any higher-order processing.

For architecture, thalamic gating determines which environmental features reach conscious processing. In a rich sensory environment (multimodal architectural space with visual, acoustic, thermal, and social inputs), the TRN gates the most salient channel(s) while suppressing others. This is complementary to, not competitive with, Friston's cortical precision weighting — thalamic gating is the subcortical pre-filter (coarse, fast, ~50ms) and cortical precision weighting is the cortical post-filter (fine, slower, ~200ms).

The evidence base is primarily from animal electrophysiology (Saalmann et al., 2012, N=2 macaques, single-unit recording in pulvinar during selective attention; showed alpha-band thalamocortical coherence predicting attentional selection with 80% accuracy). Human evidence comes from fMRI showing thalamic activation patterns consistent with gating during selective attention (Kastner et al., 2004, N=6). The template will carry MECHANISM warrant for the gating mechanism itself, but THEORETICAL_DEFAULT for the architectural instantiation.

# CRUCIBLE DEBATES (3)

## Crucible 1: Dose-Response Functional Form — Universal Principle or Domain Catalogue?

**Berman**: The empirical evidence points to at least three distinct functional forms across environmental parameters: log-linear for restorative stimuli (nature, daylight — diminishing returns), inverted-U for homeostatic parameters (temperature, perhaps noise), and near-linear for cumulative stressors at low-moderate levels. A single universal function cannot accommodate this diversity. AX_DOSE_RESPONSE_007 should specify a typology of functional forms indexed to parameter class, not a universal curve.

**Friston**: I agree that no single curve fits all, but I disagree that this requires a brute-force typology. The precision-weighting framework provides a generative principle: the functional form is determined by the shape of the brain's prior distribution over each parameter. Homeostatic parameters (temperature, metabolic state) have narrow priors → inverted-U. Information-rich parameters (nature, spatial complexity) have broad, decaying priors → log-linear or power-law. Stressor parameters (noise, crowding) have half-sided priors (more is worse) → monotonic. The typology emerges from the prior structure, which IS a universal principle even though it produces diverse curves.

**Berman**: Karl, that's elegant but underdetermined. The same prior-shape argument could predict multiple functional forms for the same parameter depending on what assumptions you make about the prior. Nature exposure could be log-linear (if you model it as uncertainty reduction) or sigmoidal (if you model it as resource restoration with a saturation point). The empirical curves must anchor the theoretical framework, not the reverse.

**Friston**: Agreed. The framework constrains the hypothesis space (it rules out functional forms that are inconsistent with the prior structure) but does not uniquely determine the form. The specific parameters must be empirically fitted. I would propose a compromise: AX_DOSE_RESPONSE_007 specifies a three-class typology (restorative/log-linear, homeostatic/inverted-U, cumulative-stressor/monotonic) with the PP prior-structure principle as the theoretical account for WHY these three classes emerge. Each class has empirically calibrated slope and inflection parameters.

**Panel consensus**: AX_DOSE_RESPONSE_007 adopts a three-class typology: (1) Restorative/information-rich parameters (nature, daylight, spatial complexity): log-linear or power-law dose-response with diminishing returns. Prior-structure account: broad, decaying priors. (2) Homeostatic parameters (temperature, perhaps humidity, postural comfort): inverted-U centred on adaptive set-point. Prior-structure account: narrow, symmetric priors. (3) Cumulative stressors (noise, crowding, allostatic load inputs): approximately monotonic at low-moderate levels, accelerating at high levels. Prior-structure account: half-sided priors. Each class specifies canonical slope/inflection parameters calibrated from domain-specific meta-analyses. The differential-mode model (C-10) maps onto this: divergent/implicit processes have broad dose-response curves (wide range of acceptable stimulation); convergent/explicit processes have narrower curves (more specific optimal stimulation).

## Crucible 2: Individual Differences — Trait Modifiers, Neurodiversity, or Context?

**DeYoung**: The Big Five are the most empirically validated individual-difference framework in psychology (Costa & McCrae, 1992; meta-analytic support from Soto & John, 2017, N > 1 million). Each dimension has a clear neurobiological substrate (DeYoung, 2010) and produces measurable moderation of environmental response. For AX_INDIVIDUAL_DIFFERENCES_008, I propose three tiers of individual-difference parameters: (1) Big Five trait moderators (well-calibrated, d = 0.20-0.40 per trait-environment interaction), (2) Sensory processing sensitivity and attentional profile (moderate evidence, d = 0.30-0.50, per Aron et al., 2012), (3) Clinical neurodiversity (limited population-level calibration, THEORETICAL_DEFAULT).

**Schooler**: I challenge the trait-modifier approach. Ecological rationality predicts that individual differences are context-dependent — the same person uses different heuristics (and shows different environmental sensitivities) depending on the information structure of the environment. A neurotic person in a familiar, controllable environment may show no heightened stress response; the same person in an unfamiliar, uncontrollable environment shows dramatic amplification. The AX4 interaction (perceived control) is more predictive than the trait alone. Individual differences should be modelled as trait × environment interactions, not trait main effects.

**DeYoung**: That's fair but practically intractable — the number of trait × environment interaction terms explodes combinatorially. The CMR already captures the most important interactions through AX4 (perceived control × neuroticism is essentially what the Whitehall II findings show). What AX_INDIVIDUAL_DIFFERENCES_008 should add is the trait main effects that shift dose-response curves, plus flags for the neurodiversity dimensions that produce qualitatively different processing (not just shifted curves but different functional forms — e.g., sensory processing sensitivity may convert a log-linear curve to a step function with a hard threshold).

**Panel consensus**: AX_INDIVIDUAL_DIFFERENCES_008 specifies three tiers: Tier 1 (Big Five trait moderators): shift dose-response curve parameters by d_trait (neuroticism: shift LEFT by d = 0.25-0.40 on stressor axes; extraversion: shift RIGHT by d = 0.20-0.35 on stimulation axes; openness: shift RIGHT by d = 0.15-0.30 on complexity axes). Tier 2 (Sensory processing sensitivity + attentional profile): SPS affects ALL sensory dose-response curves with narrower tolerance band (estimated 30-50% reduction in optimal range width, THEORETICAL_DEFAULT); ADHD-related NE variance modifies optimal arousal level for LC-NE explore/exploit (NM5). Tier 3 (Clinical neurodiversity): ASD, misophonia, anxiety disorders produce qualitatively different functional forms — flagged as THEORETICAL_DEFAULT with explicit note that universal design requires accommodating these populations even without precise calibration. Individual differences interact with AX4 (perceived control) — high perceived control reduces inter-individual variance (convergence toward comfortable range when control is available).

## Crucible 3: Thalamic Gating vs. Cortical Precision — Complementary or Redundant?

**Saalmann**: Thalamic gating is the first filter. The TRN receives cortical feedback (top-down attention signals from PFC) and sensory input (bottom-up signals from thalamic relay nuclei), computing a gating signal that selectively amplifies or suppresses sensory channels. This operates at ~50ms — faster than any cortical precision-weighting mechanism. In architecture: when you enter a new space, the thalamus selects which sensory channel gets priority (visual for a dramatically lit space, acoustic for a noisy café, thermal for a cold warehouse) within 50ms, before cortical processing has begun. This IS the environmental filter.

**Friston**: The thalamic gate and cortical precision weighting are complementary but hierarchically ordered. The thalamus provides coarse channel selection (which modality gets cortical access), while cortical precision weighting provides fine-grained within-channel selection (which features within the admitted modality get processing priority). They are not redundant — they operate at different timescales and spatial scales. The thalamus selects "attend to vision, suppress audition"; cortical precision weighting selects "within vision, attend to the unusual material texture, suppress the familiar wall colour."

**Saalmann**: Agreed. And the alpha-band thalamocortical coherence mechanism (Saalmann et al., 2012) provides the neural communication channel: the pulvinar synchronises alpha oscillations between thalamus and cortex, and the phase of these oscillations gates sensory transmission. When thalamic alpha and cortical alpha are in-phase, information passes; when anti-phase, information is blocked. This is a mechanism, not a metaphor — it is measurable, manipulable, and predictive.

**Friston**: The precision-weighting implementation IS this alpha-phase mechanism, described at a different level of analysis. The computational variable (precision) maps onto the neural variable (alpha-band thalamocortical coherence). High precision = in-phase = gate open. Low precision = anti-phase = gate closed. They are the same mechanism described at different levels of the Marr hierarchy.

**Panel consensus**: Thalamic gating and cortical precision weighting are complementary mechanisms operating at different timescales and spatial scales. CROSS_THALAMIC_ENVIRONMENTAL_FILTER_001 specifies the subcortical pre-filter (coarse channel selection, ~50ms, alpha-band thalamocortical coherence). This is upstream of and feeds into the cortical precision-weighting mechanism formalised in the PP framework (used throughout the CMR pipeline). The two mechanisms share a common neural substrate (alpha-band oscillations) described at different levels of analysis (neural in thalamic gating, computational in precision weighting). Partial-out rule (C-04): CROSS_THALAMIC_ENVIRONMENTAL_FILTER_001 owns the SUBCORTICAL gating mechanism; MULTIMODAL_PE_INTEGRATION_001 (NEUROMOD-I) owns the CORTICAL precision-weighted integration. No double-counting.
