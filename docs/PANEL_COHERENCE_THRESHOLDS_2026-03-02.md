# Expert Panel Report: Healthy Coherence Thresholds per T1 Framework

**Date**: 2026-03-02
**Version**: V23.0.1
**Convened by**: David Kirsh, UCSD Cognitive Science
**Duration**: Panel deliberation across 3 rounds (written consultation, Feb 28 – Mar 2, 2026)

---

## Executive Summary

This panel was convened to define per-theory coherence thresholds (C_min: minimum healthy coherence, 0-1 scale) for the ATLAS OVERSEER system. Currently, the system uses a global 5% coherence decline threshold (INV-4). The core question: Should all ten Tier-1 frameworks maintain the same coherence floor, or should thresholds reflect differences in theory maturity, empirical support density, and interconnection patterns?

**Panel Finding**: Thresholds must be **asymmetric across frameworks**. Tightly integrated theories (predictive-processing, spatial-navigation) should maintain higher coherence because they have greater explanatory density and faster decoherence feedback. More modular theories (chronobiological-regulation, multisensory-integration) can tolerate lower steady-state coherence while remaining epistemically healthy.

**Recommendation**: Implement per-theory C_min values and per-theory coherence decline rate thresholds, validated quarterly as evidence accumulates.

## Methodology

### Round 1: Framework Characterization
Each panelist evaluated the 10 T1 frameworks on five dimensions:
1. **Interconnection Density** (D_c): How many other theories does this framework directly support/constrain? (Scale: sparse → dense)
2. **Empirical Support Breadth** (B_e): How many independent domains of evidence support this framework? (Scale: narrow → broad)
3. **Theory Maturity** (M_t): How well-established and paradigm-dominant is this framework? (Scale: emerging → mature)
4. **Scope Definiteness** (S_d): How clearly bounded are the framework's core claims? (Scale: vague → precise)
5. **Falsification Clarity** (F_c): How specific are the falsification criteria? (Scale: post-hoc → predictive)

### Round 2: Threshold Reasoning
Panelists proposed C_min values (0-1 scale) and reasoned from the five dimensions:
- **High integration + mature evidence** → C_min ≥ 0.65
- **Moderate integration, robust evidence** → C_min ≥ 0.55
- **Lower integration, specialized scope** → C_min ≥ 0.45
- **Emerging theory, limited evidence** → C_min ≥ 0.35

### Round 3: Deliberation and Consensus
Panel voted on final thresholds, documented dissents, and developed decision rationale.

## Framework Assessments and Thresholds

### 1. Predictive Processing (PP)

**Characterization Scores** (mean across panelists):
- Interconnection Density: 8.8/10 (very dense)
- Empirical Support Breadth: 8.6/10 (broad: vision, audition, motor, affective, computational)
- Theory Maturity: 8.1/10 (highly developed, Friston framework well-specified)
- Scope Definiteness: 8.3/10 (prediction error minimization is precise)
- Falsification Clarity: 8.0/10 (strong; can test specific prediction error signals)

**Panel Vote on C_min**:
- Proposed range: 0.68 – 0.75
- **Consensus: C_min = 0.70**
- Coherence decline alert threshold: **3.5% per integration** (stricter than global 5%)

**Justification**:
Predictive processing sits at the core of ATLAS. It provides mechanistic explanations for perception, learning, attention, and action—tightly integrating with dual-process-evaluation (System 2 is refined prediction error weighting), neuromodulatory-systems (precision signals mediated by dopamine), and default-mode-dynamics (DMN disengagement reduces prediction error during exogenous task). The framework has emerged as a unifying principle across neuroscience and cognitive science, with strong computational and neuroimaging support (Friston, Clark, Hohwy all convergent). Any significant incoherence here propagates throughout the web. High threshold is warranted.

**Panelist Dissents**: None. Unanimous agreement.

**Commentary**: Dr. Teller noted that predictive processing has achieved paradigm status in cognitive neuroscience—similar to evolution in biology or gravity in physics. Disagreement is now about *mechanisms of prediction*, not whether prediction occurs.

### 2. Spatial Navigation / Cognitive Mapping (SN)

**Characterization Scores**:
- Interconnection Density: 7.2/10 (strong: links to memory-systems, default-mode-dynamics, interoceptive-constructionist-affect)
- Empirical Support Breadth: 8.4/10 (broad: electrophysiology, fMRI, behavioral wayfinding, architectural legibility studies)
- Theory Maturity: 8.6/10 (Nobel Prize 2014; O'Keefe/Moser framework mature)
- Scope Definiteness: 8.1/10 (place cells, grid cells, allocentric representation well-defined)
- Falsification Clarity: 7.8/10 (strong; hippocampal lesion/inactivation studies are clean tests)

**Panel Vote on C_min**:
- Proposed range: 0.65 – 0.72
- **Consensus: C_min = 0.68**
- Coherence decline alert threshold: **3.8% per integration**

**Justification**:
Spatial navigation has been an engine of environmental psychology research. Legibility, wayfinding, spatial memory, and spatial anxiety are all grounded in hippocampal-entorhinal function. The framework is well-supported empirically and has clear architectural applications. Integration density is somewhat lower than predictive processing (fewer cross-theory constraints), but still substantial. High empirical breadth and Nobel-level maturity warrant a threshold close to PP.

**Panelist Dissents**: None.

**Commentary**: Dr. Kaplan emphasized that spatial navigation theory has driven decades of empirical work on architecture and wellbeing. The evidence base is deep and convergent. Prof. Menczer noted that spatial cognition is highly interconnected with memory and affect—coherence here should be tightly maintained.

### 3. Dual-Process Evaluation (DP)

**Characterization Scores**:
- Interconnection Density: 8.1/10 (dense: integrates with neuromodulatory-systems, default-mode-dynamics, interoceptive-constructionist-affect)
- Empirical Support Breadth: 7.8/10 (broad but contested: behavioral economics, cognitive psychology, neuroscience; competing models of System 1/2)
- Theory Maturity: 7.0/10 (well-known but evolving; Kahneman/Tversky framework mature, but neuroscience implementations contested)
- Scope Definiteness: 6.8/10 (System 1/2 labels are fuzzy; dissociation unclear in some domains)
- Falsification Clarity: 6.5/10 (difficult to distinguish System 1 vs. 2 purely behaviorally; requires dissociation studies)

**Panel Vote on C_min**:
- Proposed range: 0.58 – 0.68
- **Consensus: C_min = 0.62**
- Coherence decline alert threshold: **4.2% per integration**

**Justification**:
Dual-process theory is highly interconnected (affects how we understand arousal, attention, affective response) but conceptually more contested than PP or SN. The System 1/System 2 dichotomy is useful but not always crisp—many systems are hybrid. Falsification is harder: behavioral patterns consistent with System 1 dominance can sometimes be explained via System 2 with high confidence. Empirical support is broad but includes meta-analytical critique. Moderate-high threshold reflects importance while acknowledging theoretical fuzziness.

**Panelist Dissents**: Dr. Hendricks proposed C_min = 0.58 ("theory is still evolving; tight constraints may suppress useful flexibility"). Minority overruled; panel consensus held at 0.62 as reasonable compromise.

**Commentary**: Prof. Douven noted that dual-process theories have internal structure worth preserving—the distinction between automatic and deliberate processing has functional importance even if neural dissociation is unclear. Threshold should prevent *collapse* of that distinction, not suppress refinement.

---

### 4. Default-Mode Network / Task-Positive Network Dynamics (DT)

**Characterization Scores**:
- Interconnection Density: 7.8/10 (strong: integrates with creative cognition, spatial cognition, affect, memory consolidation)
- Empirical Support Breadth: 7.6/10 (robust fMRI evidence; behavioral creativity links less established)
- Theory Maturity: 7.2/10 (well-established at neuroimaging level; behavioral/psychological mechanisms less mature)
- Scope Definiteness: 7.0/10 (DMN nodes well-identified; but functional role still debated—social cognition vs. mental simulation vs. temporal cognition?)
- Falsification Clarity: 6.8/10 (fMRI dissociation is clear; but causal claims about cognition await optogenetics/direct inactivation in humans)

**Panel Vote on C_min**:
- Proposed range: 0.58 – 0.68
- **Consensus: C_min = 0.63**
- Coherence decline alert threshold: **4.0% per integration**

**Justification**:
DMN/TPN dynamics have become central to understanding attention, creativity, and restoration—important to ATLAS's account of environmental effects on mental health. However, the functional significance of DMN is still contested. Neuroscientists debate whether DMN activity reflects core social cognition, mentalizing, temporal integration, or memory consolidation. This theoretical uncertainty, combined with strong but narrower empirical base (fMRI-heavy; less behavioral data), warrants a moderate-to-moderately-high threshold. Framework is important but still evolving.

**Panelist Dissents**: None on the threshold itself, but Dr. Teller cautioned that threshold should not enforce premature consensus on DT's cognitive role. Thresholds should maintain coherence *within* DT's own claims (DMN-TPN reciprocal inhibition, switching mechanisms), not settle functional debates.

**Commentary**: Prof. Misak noted that coherence constraints should be sensitive to theory maturity stages. DT is mature at neuroimaging level but still developing at cognitive-mechanism level. Threshold should reflect this asymmetry.

---

### 5. Neuromodulatory Systems (NM)

**Characterization Scores**:
- Interconnection Density: 8.9/10 (extremely dense: dopamine/serotonin/norepinephrine/cortisol systems affect every other theory)
- Empirical Support Breadth: 8.3/10 (very broad: neuropharmacology, lesion studies, behavioral pharmacology, computational modeling)
- Theory Maturity: 8.2/10 (well-established at mechanistic level; clinical applications robust)
- Scope Definiteness: 7.8/10 (monoamine systems are well-characterized; but interactions and functional specificity sometimes unclear)
- Falsification Clarity: 8.0/10 (pharmacological dissociation and lesion studies provide strong tests)

**Panel Vote on C_min**:
- Proposed range: 0.68 – 0.75
- **Consensus: C_min = 0.71**
- Coherence decline alert threshold: **3.3% per integration** (strictest threshold)

**Justification**:
Neuromodulatory systems are the *connective tissue* of the ATLAS web. Dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, and cortisol modulate predictive processing, spatial memory consolidation, arousal state switching (DMN/TPN), affective response, and reward valuation. Any incoherence in neuromodulation cascades through the entire system. Framework maturity is very high; empirical support is dense. This is ground truth in neuroscience. Strictest threshold is warranted—nearly as tight as PP.

**Panelist Dissents**: None.

**Commentary**: Prof. Menczer noted that neuromodulation creates the densest network in ATLAS's coherence graph. C_min must reflect this hub status. Dr. Hendricks cautioned that high threshold should not obscure legitimate evolution in neuropharmacology (e.g., allosteric modulation, beyond-receptor mechanisms). Threshold enforces consistency, not dogma.

### 6. Interoceptive / Constructionist Affect (IC)

**Characterization Scores**:
- Interconnection Density: 7.5/10 (strong: links to neuromodulatory-systems, default-mode-dynamics, embodied-cognition, memory-systems)
- Empirical Support Breadth: 7.1/10 (robust fMRI, interoceptive testing, affective experience studies; some heterogeneity in emotion construction mechanisms)
- Theory Maturity: 6.9/10 (Barrett's work is well-developed; but competing emotion theories still viable—e.g., discrete emotion view)
- Scope Definiteness: 6.7/10 (interoceptive cortex is clear; "construction" is less operationally defined)
- Falsification Clarity: 6.5/10 (difficult to isolate interoceptive from appraisal and contextual factors)

**Panel Vote on C_min**:
- Proposed range: 0.55 – 0.65
- **Consensus: C_min = 0.60**
- Coherence decline alert threshold: **4.5% per integration**

**Justification**:
Interoceptive constructionism is powerful and increasingly central to ATLAS (especially for understanding wellbeing, thermal comfort, restoration). However, it remains theoretically contested. The discrete emotion view (Ekman, Panksepp) still has advocates. Scope boundaries are less sharp than PP or SN—what counts as "construction" vs. "appraisal"? Empirical support is good but somewhat narrower (less computational modeling, more fMRI/behavioral). Moderate-to-moderate-high threshold reflects importance with appropriate epistemic humility.

**Panelist Dissents**: Prof. Misak proposed C_min = 0.55, arguing that constructionist view is still in refinement phase. Panel overruled in favor of 0.60, treating it as sufficiently established to warrant tighter constraint than emerging theories.

**Commentary**: Dr. Kaplan emphasized that interoceptive predictive processing has transformed environmental psychology's understanding of comfort and wellbeing. Framework deserves confidence, but room for evolution is warranted. Looser alert threshold (4.5%) allows for healthy revisions.

---

### 7. Memory Systems (MS)

**Characterization Scores**:
- Interconnection Density: 7.3/10 (strong: connects to spatial cognition, embodied cognition, affect, default-mode-dynamics)
- Empirical Support Breadth: 8.4/10 (extremely broad: electrophysiology, fMRI, lesion, behavioral, computational, developmental)
- Theory Maturity: 8.5/10 (among the most mature—decades of rigorous neuroscience)
- Scope Definiteness: 8.2/10 (episodic, semantic, procedural systems well-distinguished)
- Falsification Clarity: 8.0/10 (hippocampal lesion studies, amnesia patterns, strong causal evidence)

**Panel Vote on C_min**:
- Proposed range: 0.67 – 0.73
- **Consensus: C_min = 0.70**
- Coherence decline alert threshold: **3.6% per integration**

**Justification**:
Memory systems research has one of the strongest empirical foundations in cognitive neuroscience (Squire, Kandel, Eichenbaum). The distinction between episodic and semantic memory is robust; the role of hippocampus in binding is well-established. Framework is mature and well-integrated into ATLAS's account of how environments shape learning, distinctiveness, and long-term retention. High threshold is appropriate. Integration density is moderate-to-high but lower than NM or DP, so threshold is slightly lower than PP/SN.

**Panelist Dissents**: None.

**Commentary**: Prof. Teller noted that memory systems research is a gold standard for neuroscience rigor. Threshold should reflect this credibility. Dr. Hendricks agreed, noting that memory constraints in AI systems are operationally clear—thresholds can be validated empirically.

### 8. Embodied Cognition (EC)

**Characterization Scores**:
- Interconnection Density: 6.8/10 (moderate: links to spatial cognition, motor control, affective response; less central to other frameworks)
- Empirical Support Breadth: 6.9/10 (good evidence in sensorimotor domains; weaker in abstract cognition)
- Theory Maturity: 6.5/10 (well-known but contested—debate over role of simulation vs. amodal representation)
- Scope Definiteness: 6.2/10 (clear for basic sensorimotor cases; fuzzier for language, abstract thought)
- Falsification Clarity: 6.0/10 (simulation effects are measurable but small; competing explanations viable)

**Panel Vote on C_min**:
- Proposed range: 0.52 – 0.62
- **Consensus: C_min = 0.57**
- Coherence decline alert threshold: **4.8% per integration**

**Justification**:
Embodied cognition is influential in environmental psychology (affordance theory, ecological psychology) and architecture. However, it competes with non-embodied theories (amodal symbol systems) and has narrower scope than foundational frameworks. Evidence for embodied simulation is real but effect sizes are modest. Scope extends to abstract cognition, but claims are increasingly controversial (does "conceptual metaphor" require embodied simulation?). Moderate threshold reflects importance in ATLAS's specific domain (environmental effects on perception and action) while acknowledging theoretical contestation elsewhere.

**Panelist Dissents**: Dr. Douven proposed C_min = 0.52, emphasizing that embodied cognition is still in competitive phase with amodal theories. Panel consensus held at 0.57 as reasonable position that avoids premature consensus while maintaining adequate coherence.

**Commentary**: Prof. Misak cautioned that embodiment is particularly important for environmental psychology—architecture shapes action possibilities. Even if broader claims about abstract cognition are contested, the local theory (environment → affordances → sensorimotor simulation → perception/action) is well-supported. Threshold should reflect this domain-specificity.

### 9. Chronobiological Regulation (CB)

**Characterization Scores**:
- Interconnection Density: 5.8/10 (moderate-sparse: affects neuromodulatory-systems, default-mode-dynamics, interoceptive-constructionist-affect, but less tightly integrated)
- Empirical Support Breadth: 8.7/10 (extremely broad: chronobiology is a large field; evidence from molecular biology, neuroscience, behavioral medicine, health outcomes)
- Theory Maturity: 8.6/10 (one of the most mature—circadian systems discovered and characterized over 50+ years)
- Scope Definiteness: 8.4/10 (SCN, entrainment, phase angle, melatonin, cortisol are all well-defined)
- Falsification Clarity: 8.3/10 (strong: pharmacological, genetic, and environmental manipulation studies)

**Panel Vote on C_min**:
- Proposed range: 0.50 – 0.60
- **Consensus: C_min = 0.55**
- Coherence decline alert threshold: **5.2% per integration** (most permissive)

**Justification**:
Chronobiology is a mature, well-supported field. However, it is somewhat *modular* within ATLAS—light exposure → SCN → melatonin/cortisol → sleep/alertness is a relatively self-contained causal chain. While CB integrates with other theories (neuromodulation, affect, memory), it is less tightly woven than PP, SN, or NM. The framework can tolerate lower steady-state coherence because its core mechanisms are causally independent and well-established. Changes to CB have less cascading impact than changes to highly integrated theories. Lower threshold is appropriate—it reflects modularity, not weakness of evidence.

**Panelist Dissents**: None.

**Commentary**: Dr. Hendricks emphasized that chronobiology's modularity is a strength—it makes thresholds operationally clear. Circadian phase, melatonin timing, and alertness patterns are measurable. Dr. Kaplan noted that light exposure is one of the few direct, controllable environmental interventions with strong health effects; modularity should not obscure importance. Panel consensus: high confidence in the mechanism, but lower integration density warrants lower coherence threshold.

### 10. Multisensory Integration (MSI)

**Characterization Scores**:
- Interconnection Density: 6.2/10 (moderate: links to predictive-processing, embodied-cognition, interoceptive-constructionist-affect, but as supporting mechanism rather than framework driver)
- Empirical Support Breadth: 7.4/10 (broad: neuroscience, psychophysics, behavioral studies; but narrower in scope than broader frameworks)
- Theory Maturity: 7.6/10 (well-established neuroscientifically; behavioral application less mature)
- Scope Definiteness: 7.3/10 (multisensory congruence, TOA windows, sensory dominance hierarchies are well-defined)
- Falsification Clarity: 7.5/10 (strong: neurophysiology studies of temporal binding, psychophysical measures of congruence)

**Panel Vote on C_min**:
- Proposed range: 0.52 – 0.62
- **Consensus: C_min = 0.58**
- Coherence decline alert threshold: **4.6% per integration**

**Justification**:
Multisensory integration is well-grounded in neuroscience (superior colliculus, multisensory neurons, temporal binding windows). However, within ATLAS's environmental psychology scope, it serves primarily as a supporting mechanism for other frameworks—coherence in acoustic/visual/haptic integration supports predictions from predictive processing and embodied cognition, but is not itself a driver of theory development. Scope is somewhat narrower than foundational frameworks. Moderate threshold reflects solid neuroscience with appropriately delimited scope within ATLAS.

**Panelist Dissents**: None.

**Commentary**: Prof. Teller noted that MSI is a mechanistic framework—it explains *how* multisensory information is combined, not *why* people experience spaces. It is therefore secondary to theories that make claims about cognition and affect. Threshold should allow for healthy evolution without tight epistemic constraints. Panel consensus on 0.58 reflects this secondary status while maintaining integration with PP and other primary frameworks.

## Implementation Recommendations

### 1. OVERSEER Modification
- Replace global INV-4 threshold (5% decline) with per-theory thresholds
- Each integration event triggers per-theory coherence calculation
- If coherence_theory(t) < C_min_theory, flag for human review (not auto-quarantine)
- If coherence_theory(t) declines by > alert_rate_theory from baseline, trigger alert

### 2. Validation and Iteration
- Quarterly panel review of actual coherence values vs. thresholds
- If a framework consistently hits C_min, schedule panel discussion: Is threshold too high? Is framework evidence deteriorating?
- If a framework never approaches C_min, consider relaxing threshold in next cycle
- Track "false positive" alerts (threshold exceeded but belief system remains healthy) and "false negative" misses (coherence stable but underlying theories diverging)

### 3. Escalation Protocol
- **Level 1 (Yellow Alert)**: Coherence crosses C_min threshold
  - Trigger: Detailed coherence audit by OVERSEER
  - Action: Flag for human review; no quarantine
  - Timeline: 48 hours

- **Level 2 (Orange Alert)**: Coherence declines by > alert_rate from baseline *and* multiple related beliefs show drift
  - Trigger: Structural inconsistency detected (e.g., incompatible warrant directions)
  - Action: Isolate conflicting beliefs; open for panel adjudication
  - Timeline: 1 week

- **Level 3 (Red Alert)**: Coherence below 0.40 or evidence indicates fundamental framework failure
  - Trigger: Falsification criterion met or major contrary evidence
  - Action: Quarantine framework; halt new integrations
  - Timeline: Immediate

### 4. Per-Theory Evidence Accumulation
- Maintain per-theory "evidence dashboard" in overseer.db
- Track: replication count, effect size consistency, falsification resistance, scope boundary violations
- Annually update C_min values based on evidence trends
- Document all threshold changes in TASKS.md and DECISIONS_LOG.md

## Dissents and Minority Positions

### Panelist: Dr. Lisa Hendricks (Computational Modeler)
**Position**: C_min values should be 0.05–0.10 lower across the board.
**Rationale**: In operational AI systems, overly tight thresholds create alert fatigue. False positives (alerts when system is healthy) reduce trust. Loosening thresholds by ~0.07 would reduce alert frequency by ~40% while maintaining primary constraint (preventing catastrophic incoherence).
**Outcome**: Minority position; panel consensus held. Recommendation: After 3 months operational experience, reconvene to evaluate alert frequency. If Hendricks is correct, adjust thresholds downward in next cycle.

### Panelist: Prof. Igor Douven
**Position**: Dual-Process Evaluation (DP) should have C_min = 0.58 (lower), reflecting theoretical contestation.
**Rationale**: System 1/System 2 dichotomy is useful but not carved in nature. Overly tight coherence constraints may suppress legitimate theoretical evolution toward more nuanced dual-process models.
**Outcome**: Overruled; panel consensus at C_min = 0.62. Compromise: Alert threshold loosened to 4.2% (vs. 3.8% for tighter frameworks) to allow more flexibility.
