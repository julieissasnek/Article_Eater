# Part I — Reconsidering “Appraisal”

Before expanding the ontology, we must address a conceptual issue:

Are these systems truly *appraisals*?  
Or are they simply neural responses to structure?

This is not a trivial semantic question.

In classical appraisal theory (e.g., Lazarus, Scherer), appraisal refers to a cognitive evaluation of significance relative to goals or well-being. However, contemporary affective neuroscience suggests many evaluations occur pre-reflectively, without propositional thought.

Thus we adopt a layered view:

1. **Reactive processing** — low-level perceptual organization (edge detection, grouping).
2. **Constraint-based organization** — systems that regulate coherence, load, prediction.
3. **Evaluative tagging** — processes that assign valence or motivational direction.

The hubs we identified are not reflective judgments. They are **constraint-sensitive organizing systems** whose outputs are affectively valenced.

They are evaluative in the following minimal sense:

A system is evaluative if it modulates behavior or physiology in a direction-sensitive way relative to organismic goals (e.g., safety, efficiency, resource conservation, social positioning).

Thus:

- Threat detection is evaluative because it biases avoidance.
- Fluency detection is evaluative because it biases approach.
- Overload detection is evaluative because it biases withdrawal or stress activation.

They need not be conscious or propositional.

# Part IV — Why Call Them Evaluative?

A system affected by clutter or noise is reacting, not necessarily appraising in the reflective sense.

However, modern appraisal theory defines evaluation as:

- Relevance detection
- Significance computation
- Goal-relative modulation

Clutter becomes evaluative when it:

1. Elevates stress markers,
2. Alters approach–avoid behavior,
3. Changes attentional allocation.

Thus these systems are evaluative functionally, even if non-propositional.

# Part V — System Interactions

These hubs form a constraint network.

## Fluency ↔ Complexity

Moderate complexity maximizes interest when fluency remains above threshold.

## Predictability ↔ Threat

Unexpected sharp features in dim contexts increase threat salience.

## Prospect ↔ Safety ↔ Social Signaling

Large vertical atria increase safety perception and institutional authority inference.

## Restorativeness

Occurs when moderate complexity, low threat, and high coherence align.

# Appendix A — Formal Definitions

## Processing Fluency
Inverse of cognitive effort required to encode perceptual input.

## Complexity
Structural variability across scales, measurable via entropy or fractal metrics.

## Predictability
Degree to which perceptual input conforms to probabilistic expectations.

## Threat Appraisal
Magnitude of avoidance-biased activation triggered by environmental features.

## Prospect
Extent of unobstructed visual access outward.

## Refuge
Degree of protection from exposure.

## Restorativeness
Capacity to reduce cognitive fatigue and physiological stress.

## Affordance Fit
Clarity of action possibilities relative to goals.

## Social Signaling
Degree to which architecture conveys culturally recognized status information.

The ontology decomposes aesthetic response into measurable structural variables and interacting evaluative constraint systems.
