DESIGN DECISIONS AND RATIONALES
================================================================================

Decision 1: Use Gaussian (normal distribution) for P(x) = exp(−(C−C*)²/(2σ²))
Rationale: Symmetric, mathematically tractable, fits published data, parsimonious

Decision 2: Direct labeling instead of legends (where feasible)
Rationale: Reduces cognitive load, improves accessibility, follows Tufte principles

Decision 3: Viridis-based color palette
Rationale: Perceptually uniform, colorblind-safe, high luminance variation

Decision 4: SVG format (not PNG/PDF)
Rationale: Vector-based (scalable), text preserved as selectable content, smaller file size, publication-ready

Decision 5: Small-multiples over faceted single plot (Figure 3)
Rationale: Allows comparison of similar patterns across modalities, standard in cognitive psychology

Decision 6: Three-layer boxology (Figure 5)
Rationale: Shows functional abstraction (top), neural instantiation (middle), theoretical grounding (bottom)

Decision 7: Shaded Goldilocks zones on all cross-modal figures
Rationale: Visually highlights optimal region, makes practical implications clear

Decision 8: Include error/scatter points in preference curves
Rationale: Acknowledges measurement variability, prevents overfitting interpretation

Decision 9: No 3D effects or gradients
Rationale: Tufte principle: reduces ink per data unit, improves clarity and accessibility

Decision 10: Scientific American caption style
Rationale: Self-contained, interpretable without reading full paper, professional standard
