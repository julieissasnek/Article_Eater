## What This Corpus Is

The Article Eater template corpus is an AI-assisted evidence synthesis covering architecture-wellbeing relationships. It contains 73+ mechanistic templates organized across 10 architectural domains, produced through a structured panel methodology, and grounded in a Predictive Processing (PE) theoretical framework.

## The Panel Methodology

### What the Panels Are

Each panel convenes a group of 6–15 named researchers as virtual participants. The panel format follows a structured sequence: position statements, moderated debates on contested claims, consensus calibration of quantitative parameters, and prospectus for future work. The panels are attributed to real scientists whose published work grounds the claims made.

### What the Panels Are NOT

**The panels are AI-generated evidence syntheses, not transcripts of actual expert consultations.** No named researcher was contacted, consulted, or asked to endorse the positions attributed to them. The panels were produced by an AI system (Claude/Opus, Anthropic) working under the direction of David Kirsh, Professor of Cognitive Science at UCSD.

### How Attributions Work

When the corpus states that a named researcher "provides" a finding or "argues" a position, this should be understood as:

**Category A — Direct from published work.** The claim is stated or directly supported in the researcher's published papers. These claims carry standard APA citations and can be verified against the cited publications. The majority of parameter values and effect sizes fall in this category.

**Category B — Consistent extrapolation.** The claim extends the researcher's published work in a direction consistent with their research program, but the specific parameter estimate or application context goes beyond what they have published. Example: if a researcher has published noise effects on creativity at 70 dB, and the panel estimates the effect at 65 dB by interpolation, that is a Category B extrapolation. These are marked in the templates with maturity ratings of "preliminary" or "expert estimate."

**Category C — AI-generated synthesis.** The claim represents the AI system's integration of multiple researchers' work to produce a novel parameter estimate, interaction model, or theoretical synthesis. The convergence triad channel weights, the CREA2 interaction matrix cells not directly measured, and the lifespan U-curve moderators are examples. These are marked with maturity ratings of "preliminary" or "speculative."

### Implications for Use

1. **Category A claims** can be cited as "[Original Author], as reviewed in [this corpus document]."
2. **Category B claims** should be cited as "estimated by extrapolation from [Original Author]'s work; see [this corpus document] for methodology."
3. **Category C claims** should be cited as "AI-assisted synthesis in [this corpus document], drawing on [list of contributing researchers]; not independently validated."
4. **No claim in this corpus should be attributed to a named researcher as their personal statement** unless it is a direct quotation from their published work with a standard citation.

## The PE Framework

The Predictive Processing (PE) framework serves as the theoretical spine of the corpus. Its contribution varies across templates (see Doc 67 Part 2 for the full classification):

- **Predictive** (~30% of templates): PE generates novel quantitative predictions that differ from what the empirical literature alone provides.
- **Explanatory** (~38%): PE explains known empirical findings mechanistically, providing the "why" behind established "what" findings.
- **Organizational** (~32%): PE provides consistent vocabulary and taxonomic structure without adding explanatory power beyond the underlying empirical research.

The corpus would be weaker without the PE framework — it provides genuine explanatory and predictive value in the majority of templates and essential organizational coherence across all of them. However, the PE contribution should not be overstated: many templates function as parameterized evidence compendia that would retain their practical value under any theoretical framework.

## Calibration and Validation Status

### What Has Been Calibrated

Nine of ten template series have undergone calibration panels that provide quantitative parameters: boundary values, channel weights, Goldilocks zones, interaction coefficients, and lifespan moderators. These parameters are grounded in published empirical research (Category A) and expert extrapolation (Category B).

### What Has NOT Been Validated

**No parameter in this corpus has been validated in an actual built environment.** All calibration data derives from laboratory studies, field surveys, or expert extrapolation. The ecological validity of the parameter predictions — whether the Goldilocks zones, channel weights, and interaction coefficients hold when real occupants inhabit real buildings — is unknown.

This is the corpus's most significant limitation. The system should be understood as a hypothesis-generating framework that produces specific, testable, quantitative predictions about architecture-wellbeing relationships, not as a validated assessment tool.

### Coverage Ratings

Domain coverage is rated on a defined rubric:

| Level | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| ★ | Domain identified; no templates |
| ★★ | Templates exist, uncalibrated |
| ★★★ | Calibrated with quantitative parameters; expert estimates; Goldilocks boundaries |
| ★★★★ | Calibrated with empirical data; some architectural-context validation |
| ★★★★★ | End-to-end validated in built environments |

Current honest ratings average ★★★ to ★★★½ across 10 domains. Two domains (A4 Light, A8 Social Configuration) approach ★★★★ based on the depth of empirical grounding. No domain has reached ★★★★★.
