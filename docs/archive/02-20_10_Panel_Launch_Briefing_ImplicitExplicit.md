# Launch Briefing: Implicit–Explicit Processing (Type 1 / Type 2) — New T1 Theory Panel
## For new Opus chat — February 20, 2026

---

## TASK

Convene an expert panel to develop a **new Tier 1 theory** for the Goldilocks/CMR system based on the distinction between implicit (Type 1) and explicit (Type 2) processing in environmental response. The theory should:

1. Articulate the dual-process distinction as it applies to architectural/environmental experience
2. Identify the neural mechanisms (T2 level)
3. Generate testable templates with architectural predictions
4. Specify what this framework explains that PP, Berlyne, ART, Biophilia, and SRT cannot
5. Reconcile Kirsh's representational sense of implicit/explicit with Kahneman's processing sense

---

## THE PROBLEM (developed in prior session)

All current Tier 1 frameworks in the CMR system are fundamentally **implicit/subpersonal**:

- **PP**: Prediction error is computed below conscious awareness. The dark room problem emerges because PP cannot represent explicit goals overriding PE minimization.
- **Berlyne**: Arousal potential is a stimulus property, not a cognitive evaluation. Collative variables are measured at the stimulus, not the person's reflective response.
- **SRT**: Explicitly built to bypass cognitive appraisal — Ulrich's core claim is a "pre-cognitive affective pathway." If you let explicit processing in, SRT loses its theoretical distinctiveness.
- **ART**: Depends on involuntary "soft fascination." Restoration works BECAUSE it doesn't require deliberate effort. Adding explicit processing undermines the mechanism.
- **Biophilia**: Evolved affiliations operating at autonomic level.

But adult environmental experience involves **BOTH channels**:

- **Implicit**: Autonomic responses, affective valence, PE processing, attentional capture
- **Explicit**: Aesthetic judgment, semantic meaning ("this hospital looks like a place where people die"), cultural norms, deliberate engagement, activity frame selection

### The Four-Step Bridge Mechanism (from the Activity Space topic page)

1. Activity selection is **EXPLICIT** (deliberate, prefrontal, goal-directed)
2. Activity frame reconfigures affordance ecology (epistemic bubble)
3. Reconfiguration is largely **IMPLICIT** (task-positive network, attentional filtering)
4. Effective stimulus changes → PE-based processing operates over different input → Goldilocks optimum shifts

### Key Claim

The Goldilocks optimum for any environmental channel is **activity-relative**: the same physical room generates different inverted-U profiles depending on the activity frame. This means explicit processing (activity selection, aesthetic judgment, metacognitive override) is not a nuisance variable — it is structurally constitutive of the environmental response.

## DAVID KIRSH'S PUBLISHED WORK ON IMPLICIT/EXPLICIT

**CRITICAL: The new chat should look up and READ these before convening the panel.** Kirsh has written on implicit vs. explicit in a **different sense** from Kahneman. For Kirsh, the distinction is representational/computational (cost of extracting information), not processing-mode (fast/automatic vs. slow/deliberate). The panel needs to reconcile these senses.

### Papers to Upload (David: these are the files to include)

1. **Activity Space paper** (Kirsh, 2026) — `__Kirsh_Title_page_final_activity_space_July.docx`
   - Three-level framework (logical/physical/cognitive), epistemic bubble, cognitive congeniality, enactive envelopes
   - The foundational paper for Activity Space as T1 theory
   - Key concept: people always engage space through an activity; the epistemic bubble is personally constructed

2. **"Problem Solving and Situated Cognition"** (Kirsh, 2009)
   - In Robbins & Aydede (Eds.), *The Cambridge Handbook of Situated Cognition*, pp. 264–306. Cambridge University Press.
   - DOI: 10.1017/CBO9780511816826.015
   - Key: problems arise in concrete settings where features of "activity spaces" affect how the problem is represented and framed. Argues situated cognition doesn't have a theory of problem solving to compete with the classical view — but offers affordances, hints, thinking with things, self-cueing.
   - ~2,900+ downloads (PhilArchive)

### Papers the New Chat Should Look Up on Google Scholar

3. **"Implicit and Explicit Representation"** (Kirsh, 2003)
   - *Encyclopedia of Cognitive Science*, 2:478–481.
   - PDF: https://adrenaline.ucsd.edu/kirsh/Articles/Implicit_Explicit/implicit_explicit.pdf
   - Key thesis: explicitness is about computational cost of recovering information, not a binary. A continuum. This is the REPRESENTATIONAL sense of implicit/explicit — different from Kahneman's PROCESSING sense.

4. **"Knowledge, Explicit vs Implicit"** (Kirsh, 2009)
   - In Bayne, Cleeremans & Wilken (Eds.), *The Oxford Companion to Consciousness*, pp. 397–402.
   - Distinguishes explicit knowledge (can be elicited, made conscious, articulated) from implicit knowledge (cannot be elicited, cannot be made conscious). Cites Polanyi: "we know more than we can say."

5. **"When Is Information Explicitly Represented?"** (Kirsh, 1990/1992)
   - *The Vancouver Studies in Cognitive Science*, pp. 340–365. Oxford University Press.
   - The earliest Kirsh paper on this topic. Computation as making explicit what was implicit. ~200+ citations.

6. **"The Intelligent Use of Space"** (Kirsh, 1995)
   - *Artificial Intelligence*, 73(1–2), 31–68. ~1,600+ citations (GS).
   - Three categories: spatial arrangements that simplify choice, simplify perception, simplify internal computation. Foundational for epistemic actions.
   - Critical for panel: shows how the ARRANGEMENT of the environment (an explicit choice) shapes implicit cognitive processing.

7. **"On Distinguishing Epistemic from Pragmatic Actions"** (Kirsh & Maglio, 1994)
   - *Cognitive Science*, 18(4), 513–549. ~2,000+ citations (GS).
   - Epistemic actions change the world to simplify internal computation. Pragmatic actions change the world to achieve a goal. This distinction maps directly onto the implicit/explicit bridge.

8. **"Adapting the Environment Instead of Oneself"** (Kirsh, 1996)
   - *Adaptive Behavior*, 4(3–4), 415–452.
   - People actively reshape their environment to reduce cognitive load — a form of explicit environmental engagement that modulates implicit processing.

9. **"Thinking with External Representations"** (Kirsh, 2010)
   - *AI & Society*, 25(4), 441–454. ~700+ citations.
   - Seven ways external representations enhance cognitive power. The core of the extended mind argument for environmental design.

10. **"Embodied Cognition and the Magical Future of Interaction Design"** (Kirsh, 2013)
    - *ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction*, 20(1), Article 30.
    - Bridges embodied cognition to design practice.

11. **"Metacognition, Distributed Cognition and Visual Design"** (Kirsh, 2005)
    - Argues cognition and metacognition are on a continuum and both are highly interactive. The environment's visual and interactive structure facilitates metacognition — explicit monitoring of implicit processes.

### The Key Tension for the Panel

Kirsh's implicit/explicit = **representational cost** (how much computation to extract information)
Kahneman's Type 1/Type 2 = **processing mode** (automatic/fast vs. deliberate/slow)

But in architectural experience, BOTH senses are active simultaneously:
- A chef's mise-en-place is **explicit** in Kirsh's sense (deliberately arranged, information made spatially accessible) but operates **implicitly** in Kahneman's sense (reduces cognitive load so System 1 handles sequencing)
- A grad student persisting through hard material is **explicit** in Kahneman's sense (System 2 override of System 1's desire to disengage) but the material she's studying may be **implicit** in Kirsh's sense (the deep structure of the math is not yet computationally accessible to her)

The new T1 theory needs to handle BOTH senses and specify which sense is operative in which architectural phenomena.

## PANEL PROCEDURE (standard for this project)

1. **Position statements**: Each panelist states their position and what they bring (focus on how their work addresses the implicit/explicit distinction in environmental contexts)
2. **Identify candidate phenomena**: Architectural situations where the implicit/explicit distinction matters (at least 8–10 concrete examples)
3. **Debate**: For each phenomenon, what can/cannot be explained by existing T1 frameworks
4. **Identify unique explanatory territory**: What does the implicit/explicit theory explain that no existing framework covers
5. **Propose T2 mechanisms**: Neural substrates (prefrontal control, ACC conflict monitoring, default mode / task-positive network switching, dlPFC aesthetic judgment, etc.)
6. **Draft templates**: T_IE_001, T_IE_002, etc. with quantitative predictions and moderating variables
7. **Identify disagreements**: Mark explicitly where the panel cannot reach consensus
8. **Consensus synthesis**: T1 theory specification with formal structure
