## Position Statements

### D1: HUE × CONTEXT INTERACTION MATRIX

**PS-Elliot: The Context × Hue Matrix**

Color-in-Context research (Elliot & Maier, 2012; Elliot et al., 2007, ~1,800 citations) establishes that the SAME hue produces different effects depending on evaluative context. Red impairs achievement performance (d ≈ −0.35) but enhances attractiveness ratings (d ≈ +0.40). No hue has a fixed effect.

Hue × Context matrix (d-equivalents for moderate saturation vs. white baseline):

| Hue | Calming (bedroom) | Focus (office) | Social (restaurant) | Active (gym) | Clinical (hospital) |
|-----|-------------------|----------------|---------------------|--------------|---------------------|
| **Red** | −0.30 | −0.35 | +0.30 | +0.40 | −0.40 |
| **Orange** | −0.15 | −0.10 | +0.35 | +0.30 | −0.15 |
| **Yellow** | −0.10 | +0.10 | +0.20 | +0.25 | +0.05 |
| **Green** | +0.25 | +0.20 | +0.15 | +0.10 | +0.30 |
| **Blue** | +0.35 | +0.25 | +0.05 | −0.15 | +0.25 |
| **Purple** | +0.15 | −0.05 | +0.20 | −0.10 | +0.10 |
| **Warm neutral** | +0.25 | +0.15 | +0.20 | 0.00 | +0.15 |
| **Cool neutral** | +0.10 | +0.20 | +0.05 | −0.10 | +0.05 |

POSITIVE = beneficial for context; NEGATIVE = detrimental.

**PS-Palmer: Ecological Valence Provides the Baseline**

EVT data (Palmer & Schloss, 2010, ~700 citations) give context-free preference:

| Hue | Ecological valence | Primary associations |
|-----|-------------------|---------------------|
| Blue | +0.80 | Clear sky, clean water |
| Green | +0.60 | Vegetation, nature |
| Cyan | +0.55 | Water, sky |
| Purple | +0.30 | Flowers (mixed) |
| Orange | +0.15 | Fruit, fire (mixed) |
| Red | +0.10 | Fruit, fire, blood (mixed) |
| Yellow | +0.05 | Sun, bile (highly mixed) |
| Dark yellow | −0.40 | Rot, disease |

Combined three-source model: **Chromatic_PE ≈ 0.40 × ecological_valence + 0.45 × context_match + 0.15 × regional_match**

**PS-Ou: Cross-Cultural Dimensional Structure Is Stable**

Our 8-country study (Ou et al., 2004, ~500 citations) found three color-emotion factors with high cross-cultural consistency: Color Activity (warm-cool × saturation, r > 0.85 across cultures), Color Weight (light-heavy, r > 0.90), Color Heat (hue warmth, r > 0.80). The dimensional PERCEPTION is near-universal; PREFERENCES differ. Implication: COL2 arousal parameters are culturally stable; COL1 contextual meaning needs cultural moderators — primarily for red (most variable) and white/black (funerary associations differ).

Cultural modifiers for the hue × context matrix:

| Culture cluster | Red modifier | White modifier | Black modifier | Blue modifier |
|----------------|-------------|---------------|---------------|--------------|
| Western | 0 (reference) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| East Asian | +0.20 (auspicious) | −0.15 (funerary) | 0 | 0 |
| South Asian | +0.15 (auspicious) | +0.10 (purity amplified) | −0.10 | 0 |
| Middle Eastern | −0.10 | +0.15 (purity) | −0.15 | +0.10 |
| Latin American | +0.10 | 0 | −0.10 | 0 |

### D2: HABITUATION TIME COURSE

**PS-Suk: Two Separate Habituation Curves**

Our temporal color work (Suk & Irtel, 2010, ~150 citations) distinguishes:

1. **Arousal habituation** (sensory adaptation): τ_arousal ≈ 2–4 hours immersive, 1–3 days accent.
2. **Hedonic habituation** (prediction updating): τ_hedonic ≈ 1–3 weeks immersive, 4–8 weeks accent.

**PS-Küller (via Mikellides): Real-Room Confirmation**

Lund real-room studies (Küller et al., 2006): Strongly colored rooms showed arousal differences from neutral for first 2–3 hours daily; mood differences persisted ~2 weeks; accent colors produced smaller but more persistent effects.

**Combined habituation parameters:**

| Configuration | τ_arousal | τ_hedonic | Post-habituation residual |
|-------------|-----------|-----------|--------------------------|
| Immersive (whole room) | 3 hours | 2 weeks | 12% of initial |
| Partial (accent wall, 20–30%) | 9 hours | 4 weeks | 25% of initial |
| Threshold accent (entry/transition) | 2–3 days | 8+ weeks | 60% of initial |
| Dynamic (LED, changing) | No habituation | No habituation | 80–100% maintained |

**Design principle**: Threshold-accent color resists habituation because each crossing is a discrete re-encounter (connects to SC3 and TP2). Dynamic color eliminates habituation entirely but requires LED infrastructure.

### D3: AROUSAL DOSE-RESPONSE

**PS-Valdez: The Arousal Function**

From our PAD mapping (Valdez & Mehrabian, 1994, ~1,500 citations), refined:

**Arousal_shift = (0.30 × S + 0.20 × B + 0.10 × H_w) × Area_factor × Habituation_factor**

Where S = saturation (0–1), B = brightness (0–1), H_w = hue warmth (−0.5 to +0.5), Area_factor scales by coverage, Habituation_factor = e^(−t/τ).

| Saturation | Accent (5–10%) | Partial (20–40%) | Immersive (60–100%) |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Low (< 0.2) | negligible | +0.05 | +0.10 |
| Moderate (0.2–0.5) | +0.05 | +0.15 | +0.30 |
| High (0.5–0.8) | +0.10 | +0.25 | +0.45 |
| Very high (> 0.8) | +0.15 | +0.35 | +0.55 |

Units: PAD arousal scale (0 = neutral, ±1 = extreme). For reference: coffee ≈ +0.35; quiet→party ≈ +0.70.

**PS-Conway: Categorical Boundary Amplification**

Neural data (Conway et al., 2010): Chromatic PE is amplified at categorical boundaries (~6 boundaries in hue circle).

Within-category change: PE ≈ 0.2 × hue_distance
Between-category change: PE ≈ 0.5 × hue_distance + 0.3 × boundary_crossing

Color change at a spatial threshold adds a channel to TP2's multi-modal boundary strength.

## Calibration Debate: Are Color Effects Large Enough?

**Elliot**: Acute d ≈ 0.15–0.40. Is the architectural investment justified?

**Panel consensus**: **Yes, because**: (a) every surface has color, so cumulative dose is enormous; (b) color is the cheapest design variable to change; (c) cost-to-benefit ratio is extremely favorable even at modest d; (d) color synergizes with light (L-series) and materials (MAT-series), amplifying effects when aligned. The modest individual effect size is offset by ubiquity and low cost.

## Calibration Session: Integrated Parameters

### COL1 Three-Source Chromatic PE (Calibrated)

**Formula**: Chromatic_PE = 0.40 × ecological_valence + 0.45 × context_match + 0.15 × regional_match

- ecological_valence: Palmer EVT (−0.4 to +0.8)
- context_match: Elliot hue × context d-value (−0.40 to +0.40)
- regional_match: 0 congruent; −0.15 to −0.50 incongruent
- Cultural modifier: applied to context_match per Ou's cluster data

**Categorical boundary bonus**: PE_total = PE_base × (1 + 0.5 × n_category_crossings)

This connects to TP2: color category change at a threshold adds a channel to multi-modal PE.

### COL2 Arousal Dose-Response (Calibrated)

**Formula**: Arousal = (0.30S + 0.20B + 0.10H_w) × Area × e^(−t/τ)

**Area factors**: Accent 0.30, Partial 0.65, Immersive 1.00

**Context-appropriate arousal targets:**

| Context | Target arousal | Optimal color strategy |
|---------|---------------|----------------------|
| Sleep/rest | 0.00 to −0.10 | Low saturation, cool-neutral, dim |
| Calm focus | +0.10 to +0.20 | Low-moderate saturation, blue-green, moderate brightness |
| Active work | +0.20 to +0.35 | Moderate saturation, warm-neutral accents |
| Social/dining | +0.25 to +0.40 | Moderate saturation, warm hues, varied brightness |
| Exercise | +0.35 to +0.50 | High saturation, warm hues, high brightness |

**Arousal PE** = |Actual_arousal − Target_arousal|. When > 0.20 units: noticeable discomfort. When > 0.40: significant performance/wellbeing impact.

### Lighting × Color Interaction (Joint with L-series)

CCT of illuminant shifts perceived surface color:

| Light CCT | Warm surfaces (red-orange-yellow) | Cool surfaces (blue-green) | Neutral surfaces |
|-----------|----------------------------------|--------------------------|-----------------|
| 2700K (warm) | Amplified +20% saturation perceived | Muted −15% | Warm-shifted |
| 4000K (neutral) | Reference | Reference | Reference |
| 5500K+ (cool) | Muted −15% | Amplified +15% | Cool-shifted |

The Kruithof effect applies: warm light + warm surfaces = comfortable; cool light + cool surfaces = comfortable; warm light + cool surfaces = uncomfortable; cool light + warm surfaces = uncomfortable. This produces a 2×2 comfort matrix that should be encoded alongside L1/L4 parameters.

## Testable Predictions

### Prediction 1: Hue × Context Interaction (COL1)

**Protocol**: 5 hues × 4 contexts in VR (N=120). Measure: mood, task performance, preference.
**Prediction**: Context × hue interaction explains ≥ 15% of variance beyond main effects. Red negative in focus (d ≈ −0.30), positive in social (d ≈ +0.25).

### Prediction 2: Habituation Time Course (COL1/COL2)

**Protocol**: Real-room longitudinal (4 weeks). Strongly colored vs. neutral offices (N=40). Daily arousal + weekly hedonic measures.
**Prediction**: Arousal difference drops to < 20% of Day 1 value by Day 2. Hedonic difference drops to < 20% by Week 3.

### Prediction 3: Threshold Accent Persistence (COL1/TP2)

**Protocol**: Building with colored vs. neutral thresholds. 6-week occupancy (N=60). Weekly GSR at thresholds.
**Prediction**: Threshold-accent GSR maintained at ≥ 50% of Week 1 value at Week 6 (vs. < 15% for immersive color).

## Scope Exclusions

1. **Color in art/decoration**: Representational meaning beyond PE scope.
2. **Wayfinding color codes**: Functional-symbolic, not affective PE.
3. **Chromotherapy**: Not supported by evidence. Excluded.
4. **Screen/digital color**: Emission vs. reflection — different regime. Deferred.
