## Coverage Rubric (Defined)

| Level | Meaning | Criteria |
|-------|---------|----------|
| ★ | Domain identified | Templates named but not specified |
| ★★ | Templates exist, uncalibrated | Qualitative mechanisms described; no quantitative parameters |
| ★★★ | Calibrated with expert estimates | Quantitative parameters from lab studies + expert judgment; Goldilocks boundaries; interaction effects identified |
| ★★★★ | Empirically calibrated + architectural context | Parameters validated with architectural-context data; design recommendations tested in built settings |
| ★★★★★ | End-to-end validated | Post-occupancy evaluation confirms predictions; actionable design tool |

## What This Means

The system has solid ★★★ coverage: calibrated templates with quantitative parameters across all 10 domains. This is a genuine achievement — no other resource in environmental psychology provides this level of mechanistic specificity with quantitative boundaries across this breadth of domains.

The gap to ★★★★ is consistent across all domains: **architectural-context validation is missing.** Every parameter was derived from laboratory studies or expert extrapolation. Until predictions are tested against post-occupancy evaluation data from real buildings, the system remains a well-calibrated hypothesis generator rather than a validated assessment tool.

The two strongest domains (A4 Light, A8 Social) benefit from unusually deep empirical bases in their parent disciplines (photobiology for L2 circadian; Hall/Altman proxemics + Leesman workplace surveys for SOC2 privacy). These approach ★★★★ because the underlying evidence was generated in quasi-architectural contexts (offices, hospitals, schools).
