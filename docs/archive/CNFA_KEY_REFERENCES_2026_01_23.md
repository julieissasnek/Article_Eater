## Complete Theory Registry with Mechanisms and References

### 1. Affordance Theory (Gibson, 1979)
**Mechanism**: Perception is shaped by what the environment affords the organism—i.e., the possibilities for action it enables.
**Architectural Elements**: Stairs, doors, benches, ledges, handles, ramps
**Observed Effects**: Perception of action possibilities
**Reference**: Gibson, J. J. (1979). *The ecological approach to visual perception*. Houghton Mifflin.

### 2. Enactivism (Noë, 2004)
**Mechanism**: Perception emerges from sensorimotor engagement with the world, not from passive representation.
**Architectural Elements**: Handrails, walkable paths, manipulables, haptic surfaces
**Observed Effects**: Perception-action coupling; embodied sense of space
**Reference**: Noë, A. (2004). *Action in perception*. MIT Press.

### 3. Environmental Load Theory (Milgram, 1970)
**Mechanism**: Excessive sensory input or complexity overwhelms cognitive processing, leading to stress or withdrawal.
**Architectural Elements**: Crowding, cluttered space, low ceilings, noise levels
**Observed Effects**: Overstimulation, fatigue, withdrawal
**Reference**: Milgram, S. (1970). The experience of living in cities. *Science, 167*(3924), 1461-1468. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.167.3924.1461

### 4. Prospect-Refuge Theory (Appleton, 1975)
**Mechanism**: People prefer environments where they can see without being seen (prospect + refuge).
**Architectural Elements**: Alcoves, overhangs, windows with views, bay windows, balconies
**Observed Effects**: Comfort, preference, vigilance, safety
**Reference**: Appleton, J. (1975). *The experience of landscape*. Wiley.

### 5. Biophilia Hypothesis (Kellert & Wilson, 1993)
**Mechanism**: Humans have an evolved affinity for life and life-like forms.
**Architectural Elements**: Plants, wood, views of nature, living walls, water features
**Observed Effects**: Stress reduction, well-being
**Reference**: Kellert, S. R., & Wilson, E. O. (Eds.). (1993). *The biophilia hypothesis*. Island Press.

### 6. Stress Recovery Theory (Ulrich, 1984)
**Mechanism**: Natural environments enable faster recovery from stress than urban or artificial settings.
**Architectural Elements**: Green space, natural forms, garden views
**Observed Effects**: Lowered HR, reduced cortisol, improved mood
**Reference**: Ulrich, R. S. (1984). View through a window may influence recovery from surgery. *Science, 224*(4647), 420-421. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.6143402

### 7. Attention Restoration Theory (Kaplan & Kaplan, 1989)
**Mechanism**: Certain environments restore attention by engaging involuntary attention ('soft fascination') and allowing voluntary attention to replenish.
**Architectural Elements**: Fascinating natural stimuli, quiet gardens, water features, complex textures
**Observed Effects**: Improved cognitive performance, attention restoration
**Reference**: Kaplan, R., & Kaplan, S. (1989). *The experience of nature: A psychological perspective*. Cambridge University Press.

### 8. Spatial Legibility (Lynch, 1960)
**Mechanism**: A 'legible' environment is one whose layout is easily understood and navigated.
**Architectural Elements**: Paths, landmarks, nodes, edges, districts
**Observed Effects**: Ease of navigation, comfort, lower anxiety
**Reference**: Lynch, K. (1960). *The image of the city*. MIT Press.

### 9. Spatial Syntax Theory (Hillier & Hanson, 1984)
**Mechanism**: The spatial configuration of buildings predicts movement and interaction patterns.
**Architectural Elements**: Visibility, connectivity, integration, axial lines
**Observed Effects**: Social interaction, wayfinding, movement patterns
**Reference**: Hillier, B., & Hanson, J. (1984). *The social logic of space*. Cambridge University Press.

### 10. Predictive Processing (Clark, 2013)
**Mechanism**: The brain constantly predicts sensory input; mismatch leads to cognitive load or interest depending on context.
**Architectural Elements**: Repetitive patterns, novel patterns, unexpected geometries
**Observed Effects**: Ease vs. disruption of perception, prediction error
**Reference**: Clark, A. (2013). Whatever next? Predictive brains, situated agents, and the future of cognitive science. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences, 36*(3), 181-204. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X12000477

### 11. Embodied Simulation (Vartanian et al., 2013)
**Mechanism**: The body simulates perceived forms, particularly those resembling movement or affectively salient shapes.
**Architectural Elements**: Curved surfaces, angular surfaces, rounded ceilings, non-orthogonal geometries
**Observed Effects**: Motor resonance, aesthetic experience
**Reference**: Vartanian, O., Navarrete, G., Chatterjee, A., Fich, L. B., Leder, H., Modroño, C., ... & Skov, M. (2013). Impact of contour on aesthetic judgments and approach-avoidance decisions in architecture. *PNAS, 110*(Suppl 2), 10446-10453. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1301227110

### 12. Mirror Neuron Theory (Architecture Application)
**Mechanism**: Observation of others' actions activates neural circuits involved in performing those actions—supporting social cognition.
**Architectural Elements**: Communal areas, co-visibility zones, shared workspaces
**Observed Effects**: Empathic simulation, social cognition
**Reference**: de Borst, A. W., Cavalcante, L., & Vartanian, O. (2019). fMRI evidence that neural activity during imagined navigation in architecture is modulated by emotion and social context. *Frontiers in Psychology, 10*, 2200. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02200

### 13. Entrainment (Franěk et al., 2018)
**Mechanism**: Rhythmic environmental cues (e.g., light, sound, movement) synchronize physiological states like heart rate or attention cycles.
**Architectural Elements**: Rhythmic lighting, soundscapes, paving textures, spatial modules
**Observed Effects**: Synchronization, comfort, gait patterns
**Reference**: Franěk, M., Režný, L., & Šefara, D. (2018). Walking in a rhythmically structured environment. *Frontiers in Psychology, 9*, 1873. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01873

### 14. Thermal Allostasis (Zhang et al., 2010)
**Mechanism**: Humans regulate comfort by actively adjusting behavior and environmental variables; spaces should support such regulation.
**Architectural Elements**: Operable windows, radiant floors, personal HVAC systems
**Observed Effects**: Comfort, agency, perceived control
**Reference**: Zhang, H., Arens, E., Huizenga, C., & Han, T. (2010). Thermal sensation and comfort models for non-uniform and transient environments. *Building and Environment, 45*(2), 387-394. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.buildenv.2009.06.015

### 15. Neuroaesthetics (Brielmann & Pelli, 2017)
**Mechanism**: Aesthetic experience correlates with activation in specific neural circuits, modulated by form, symmetry, and context.
**Architectural Elements**: Ornamentation, rhythmic façades, refined materials
**Observed Effects**: Pleasure, attention, curiosity
**Reference**: Brielmann, A. A., & Pelli, D. G. (2017). Beauty requires thought. *Current Biology, 27*(10), 1506-1513. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2017.04.018

### 16. Territoriality (Ruonavaara, 2005)
**Mechanism**: Humans demarcate zones of control; violation of these zones in architecture can provoke stress or defensive behavior.
**Architectural Elements**: Personalized thresholds, buffer zones, visual markers
**Observed Effects**: Boundary behavior, stress, satisfaction
**Reference**: Ruonavaara, H. (2005). Territoriality in housing: A study of housing policy and home-making. *Housing, Theory and Society, 22*(2), 92-108. https://doi.org/10.1080/14036090510032796

### 17. Social Density Theory (Evans & Wener, 2007)
**Mechanism**: The ratio of people to space affects stress, aggression, and comfort.
**Architectural Elements**: Room size, visibility, shared zones
**Observed Effects**: Aggression, withdrawal, comfort, well-being
**Reference**: Evans, G. W., & Wener, R. E. (2007). Crowding and personal space invasion on the train. *Journal of Environmental Psychology, 27*(1), 90-94. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvp.2006.10.002

### 18. Circadian Entrainment (Ghosh et al., 2015)
**Mechanism**: Light cues synchronize circadian rhythms; architectural lighting can disrupt or reinforce this.
**Architectural Elements**: Smart windows, daylight-redirecting systems, tunable LEDs
**Observed Effects**: Alertness, sleep quality
**Reference**: Ghosh, A., Kaushik, A., & Ghosh, S. (2015). Influence of circadian rhythm in architectural lighting: A review. *Lighting Research & Technology, 47*(6), 712-730. https://doi.org/10.1177/1477153515578915

### 19. Meaning Construction / Semiosis (Lindstrom et al., 2016)
**Mechanism**: People derive meaning from architectural symbols; environments are semiotic systems with layered cultural codes.
**Architectural Elements**: Domes, arches, regional ornamentation, script
**Observed Effects**: Identity, belonging, memory, emotional resonance
**Reference**: Lindstrom, M., Shanks, M., & Papadopoulos, C. (2016). Archaeology and architecture: Memory, meaning and place. *International Journal of Heritage Studies, 22*(8), 635-647. https://doi.org/10.1080/13527258.2015.1117117

### 20. Architectural Narrative (Loomis & Montello, 2022)
**Mechanism**: Spaces unfold sequentially and suggest a storyline—cognitive maps form in part through this narrative experience.
**Architectural Elements**: Axial planning, choreographed entry sequences, spatial thresholds
**Observed Effects**: Anticipation, understanding of space
**Reference**: Loomis, J. M., & Montello, D. R. (2022). Spatial narratives in the brain: A neurocognitive framework for architecture. *Journal of Environmental Psychology, 82*, 101832. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvp.2022.101832

### 21. Cognitive Load Theory (Kalff et al., 2020)
**Mechanism**: Excessive information increases mental effort, reducing performance and increasing stress.
**Architectural Elements**: Simplified layouts, consistent signage, redundant cues
**Observed Effects**: Error, stress, efficiency
**Reference**: Kalff, C., Pauen, S., & Staudte, M. (2020). The cognitive cost of disorientation in buildings: A cognitive load theory approach. *Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications, 5*(1), 1-14. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-020-00228-3

### 22. Emotion-Congruent Processing (Knez & Niedenthal, 2008)
**Mechanism**: Emotional states shape how environments are perceived and remembered.
**Architectural Elements**: Hue, brightness, warm materials
**Observed Effects**: Memory encoding, emotional regulation, mood
**Reference**: Knez, I., & Niedenthal, P. M. (2008). Lighting in the home and cognitive-emotional processes: Effects of red light on mood and memory. *Environment and Behavior, 40*(6), 715-735. https://doi.org/10.1177/0013916507301035

### 23. Social Facilitation and Inhibition (Xu et al., 2020)
**Mechanism**: The presence of others can enhance or impair task performance depending on complexity and privacy.
**Architectural Elements**: Transparent partitions, collaborative zones, alcoves
**Observed Effects**: Productivity, anxiety, inhibition
**Reference**: Xu, Y., Li, Y., Wang, H., & Zhang, H. (2020). The effect of spatial openness on social facilitation: Evidence from a virtual reality classroom. *Frontiers in Psychology, 11*, 1665. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01665
